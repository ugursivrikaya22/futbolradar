Trabzonspor 2-0 Beşiktaş: Reis's Team Win at Papara Park, but the Goal Rush Never Came

FutbolRadar 19 October 2026

Thomas Reis's Trabzonspor beat Beşiktaş 2-0 to extend their winning run under Reis to four games. Beşiktaş had more of the ball but could not get past Onana in goal.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Papara Park enjoyed another claret-and-blue night this evening. In the closing game of Trendyol Süper Lig matchweek 8, Trabzonspor beat Beşiktaş 2-0. The goals came from Ozan Tufan in the 21st minute and Mohamed Salah in the 71st. [1][2] The match details, line-ups and minute-by-minute events are on our match page; this column tells the story behind that score.

The short summary: Beşiktaş kept the ball, Trabzonspor kept hold of the game. Thomas Reis's team gave their opponents space but did not give up the penalty area, and from the moment they went ahead they played the game at the rhythm they wanted.

Match analysis: Beşiktaş had the ball, Trabzonspor seized the moments

Trabzonspor lined up in a 4-2-3-1: Onana in goal; Pina, Nwaiwu, Savić and Cabral in defence; Ozan Tufan and Fabinho in midfield; Salah, Muçi and Aral Şimşir ahead of them; and Franculino up front. For Beşiktaş, Vincenzo Italiano played Murillo, Agbadou, Emirhan Topçu and Rıdvan Yılmaz in front of Nübel; Salih Özcan, Miretti and Olaitan in midfield; Černý and Trossard on the wings; and Vlahović up front. [2]

Both teams had players missing. Beşiktaş were without captain Orkun Kökçü and Milot Rashica. For Trabzonspor, Malinovskyi, Batagov, Okay Yokuşlu and Onuachu were injured and Melih Kabasakal was suspended. [2] Reis made six changes to the starting XI from the European game against KuPS. [3]

The first half-hour belonged to Onana. In the 9th minute he kept out Olaitan's header, and in the 17th Černý's powerful shot that deflected off a defender. [2][1] With Beşiktaş applying pressure, it was the home side who scored: in the 21st minute Ozan Tufan shot from just outside the six-yard box and the ball went past a watching Nübel into the net. [1]

After the goal the game swung between the two penalty areas. In the 31st minute, when Muçi's cross came back off Agbadou, Trabzonspor expected a handball, but no penalty was given. [1] In the 39th minute Nübel denied Franculino one-on-one. [1] The biggest moment of the first half came in the 41st minute: Onana made a stunning reflex save from Trossard's shot from the left, and Murillo's shot at the empty net from the rebound hit the post. [1] That post went a long way to deciding this game.

In the second half Beşiktaş had even more of the ball but struggled to create clear chances. Černý tried in the 58th minute, Miretti in the 64th, Trossard in the 70th; all three shots went wide. [1] Trabzonspor found the moment they were waiting for in the 71st minute: Franculino got into the box and pulled the ball back, and Salah made it two. [1] Had Nübel not saved first from Saviolo and then from Salah on the rebound in the 82nd minute, the score could have been even bigger. [1]

In terms of cards the game was physical but never out of control. The referee was Cihan Aydın. [2][4] Emirhan Topçu was booked in the 61st minute for stopping Salah by pulling his shirt. [1] According to match-centre data, Miretti (32nd) and Fabinho (33rd) were also booked in the first half.

Statistical depth: possession was Beşiktaş's, quality was Trabzonspor's

FutbolRadar match-centre data draws a clear picture. Beşiktaş had 63 per cent of the ball, made 455 passes and took 17 shots. Trabzonspor played with 37 per cent possession and made only 273 passes, but put 8 of their 13 shots on target; Beşiktaş managed only 6 on target. According to the same data set, expected goals (xG) were 2.35 for Trabzonspor and 1.08 for Beşiktaş. In other words, the score is not an unfair summary of the game.

The real difference was efficiency in the box. Eleven of Trabzonspor's shots came from inside the penalty area. Eight of Beşiktaş's 17 shots came from outside it. Beşiktaş carried the ball, but in front of Onana they mostly settled for trying from distance. Trabzonspor found more dangerous chances with less of the ball.

The result has a big effect on the table too. In the TFF table after matchweek 7, Beşiktaş were 4th on 15 points and Trabzonspor 5th on 13. Trabzonspor had conceded only 5 goals in 7 games. [5] With this win Trabzonspor moved to 16 points from 8 games and went above Beşiktaş. Having not conceded, their defensive record stayed at 5. Beşiktaş stayed on 15 points and suffered their third league defeat of the season. The current standings are on our Süper Lig standings page.

Transfer verdict: why Salah is in Trabzon

Tonight's transfer headline is one name: Mohamed Salah. Trabzonspor announced on 6 August, on the club website and via KAP, that they had signed a two-year contract with the Egyptian star. That same evening a signing ceremony was held with the fans at Papara Park. [6][7][8] Salah said that day: "I am here to win trophies for this club." [8]

Tonight's goal was the on-pitch answer to that promise. Salah, who was among the leaders of the TFF scoring chart with 7 goals before the game, took his tally to 8 with his goal against Beşiktaş. [9][1] And the goal was not just a moment of finishing. In the 61st minute Emirhan Topçu could only stop him by pulling his shirt, and in the 82nd minute the pass that put Saviolo through came from his foot. [1]

On the other side of the transfer story is a homegrown figure. With this goal, Ozan Tufan scored his 6th goal in his 16th game against Beşiktaş and ended a 191-day goal drought in the Süper Lig. [3] On a night when the big signing shone, the experienced midfielder wrote his own story too.

Reviewing the prediction: plenty of goals was wrong, an open game was right

In our pre-match column we wrote plainly that we expected a high-scoring night from this derby. Let's be honest: the score did not bear that prediction out. Two goals is not a "goal rush". The 2-0 result did not fit the trend of 3 or more goals that we had drawn from both teams' games this season.

Still, the idea behind the prediction was not entirely wrong. The game was played openly: a post, key saves from Onana and Nübel, and a string of chances in the second half. [1] The total xG of 3.43 in the match-centre data and the two teams' combined 14 shots on target describe a game whose score could easily have reached four or five goals. But in football it is goals that count, not chances; tonight two goalkeepers and a post were stronger than our prediction.

Closing: Reis's fourth win

Under Reis, Trabzonspor had beaten Galatasaray 4-0, Samsunspor 3-0 and KuPS 4-2, and now they got past Beşiktaş too, winning a fourth consecutive competitive game. [2] This run now describes a method rather than just momentum: first protect your goal, then seize the moment.

For Beşiktaş there is an important warning. Before the game Italiano said: "We need to get a good result so that what we have done so far means something." [2] That result did not come, and Crystal Palace and Başakşehir lie ahead. [2] Having the ball was not enough tonight; without Orkun in midfield, Beşiktaş need to find a way to turn that possession into clear chances.
