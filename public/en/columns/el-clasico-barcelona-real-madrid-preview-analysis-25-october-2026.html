El Clásico: Barcelona – Real Madrid Preview | Ten From Ten, or a Mourinho Brake?

FutbolRadar 25 October 2026

Barcelona's absentees are in defence, Real Madrid's in attack and in goal. This El Clásico will be decided less by the 6-point gap on paper than by how the two coaches cover for their missing players.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

The season's first El Clásico is tonight. In La Liga matchweek 10, Barcelona host Real Madrid at the Spotify Camp Nou; kick-off is 21:00 Spanish time, 23:00 Turkish time [1][2]. Because clocks go back in Western Europe tonight, the difference between the two countries is two hours from today [4]. According to La Liga's match page, the game will be refereed by Juan Martínez Munuera [3]. This column was written before the game: the score, goals and full-time verdict are added after it has been played; for now, what we have is the two teams' form, their absentees and the numbers of a 124-year rivalry.

Pre-match analysis: what does the table say?

Barcelona lead the league on 27 points with 9 wins from 9 games, 39 goals scored and 9 conceded; Real Madrid are third on 21 points with 7 wins and 2 defeats (FutbolRadar table, 19 October). The 6-point gap turns El Clásico into a turning-point game even this early in the season: if Barcelona win, the gap grows to 9 points [4]. Current table: La Liga standings.

There is a record on Barcelona's side too. According to the club's preview, if Flick's team win tonight they will become the first team in La Liga history to start a season with ten from ten; they currently share the record with the 1968-69 Real Madrid. Barcelona's 39 goals in the first 9 matchweeks are also the second-best in league history; only Sevilla's 41 in 1940 are ahead [4].

Form: Barcelona's first defeat, Real Madrid's perfect October

Barcelona started the season with 11 wins in all competitions; the first defeat came on Tuesday night, 4-2 away to Paris Saint-Germain in the Champions League [4][5]. In the two weeks before that there is a result our Galatasaray-supporting readers will remember well: Galatasaray 1-4 Barcelona. Their last two league games were Barcelona 3-0 Getafe and Real Betis 2-5 Barcelona.

Real Madrid won all four of their games in October: in the league they beat Villarreal 1-0 and Sevilla 4-1, and in the Champions League they won 4-3 away to Roma and beat Leipzig 4-1 [5]. Mourinho's team have three from three in Europe; their two league defeats came away to Betis and Atlético Madrid [4]. According to Real Madrid's official website, Mbappé is the team's top scorer with 12 goals this season; Arda Güler and Yan Diomande scored in the last two games [5].

Squad news: defence at Barcelona, attack and goal at Real Madrid

Hansi Flick's list of absentees is long: Roony Bardghji, Eric García and Andreas Christensen were already out; Dani Olmo, injured against PSG, and Jules Koundé, who has an ongoing ankle problem, were also left out of the squad [4][7]. Barcelona have no suspended players. Young full-back Xavi Espart, who finished the PSG game with ankle pain, is available and could play his first El Clásico [7]. According to the club, six players in tonight's squad could experience their first El Clásico [4].

Real Madrid's biggest absentee is Vinícius Júnior: the Brazilian received a 4-game ban after his red card against Villarreal. Rodrygo, Ferland Mendy and Raúl Asencio are injured [4]. Another absence was added today: Thibaut Courtois was left out with a flu-like illness, and Illia Voloshyn was called up in his place [8]. Lunin is also in the squad [6].

Tactics: who will hide their gaps better?

This section is opinion. Barcelona's problem is the right-back and centre-back rotation: with Koundé and Christensen out, the defenders in the squad are Cancelo, Balde, Cubarsí, Gerard Martín, Xavi Espart and Àlex Campos [7]. That is a risky equation for Flick's game, built on a high press and a high defensive line, because on the other side is an Mbappé who punishes any space he finds. PSG's four goals showed that the area behind that line can be fragile [4].

On Real Madrid's side, Vinícius's absence reduces the one-on-one threat on the left wing; but in October's four wins, Güler and Diomande carried the scoring load alongside Mbappé [5]. It would be no surprise if Mourinho came to Camp Nou with a plan of conceding the ball and looking for transitions. Let's not forget: according to Barcelona's preview, the Portuguese coach is unbeaten in his last five El Clásicos against this opponent [4]. Mourinho said at his press conference: "We are in good form, but these games are different; what you have done before does not matter" [5]; Flick said the game was "much more than three points" [6].

Our reading: Barcelona's biggest asset is the ability of the Lamine Yamal – Raphinha – Pedri triangle to keep the ball in Real Madrid's half; Real Madrid's asset is Mbappé's pace in the moments when Barcelona's defence is caught high. Courtois's absence in goal is Real Madrid's least discussed but perhaps biggest disadvantage in this game.

Transfer verdicts: two different rebuilds

Both clubs changed their squads sharply in the summer, and this El Clásico is the first real test for many of the new arrivals. Barcelona signed Rodrigo Hernández (Rodri) from Manchester City until 2030 [13] and Anthony Gordon from Newcastle United [14]; Gordon, Rodri, Cancelo, Gabriel Jesus, Adeyemi and Livaković are in tonight's squad [6]. The club also announced an agreement to extend the contract of La Masia graduate Xavi Espart until 2030 [15].

At Real Madrid, the signings of the Mourinho era dominate the squad: Denzel Dumfries from Inter (until 2030) [16], Carlos Espí from Levante (until 2031) [17] and Yan Diomande from RB Leipzig (until 2033) [18] were officially announced. Konaté, Cucurella and Bernardo Silva are also in tonight's squad [6]. Our view: Barcelona's transfers add pieces to an existing playing model, while Real Madrid's are building a new coach's team from scratch. Diomande's goals in the last two games [5] are the first sign that this investment is starting to bear fruit.

Statistics: perfect balance over 264 games

El Clásico's official record is perfectly level: in 264 official games both teams have 106 wins each, and 52 games ended in draws. Real Madrid lead 444-441 on goals [10][12]. In La Liga alone, across 192 games Real Madrid have 80 wins and Barcelona 77, with 35 draws; on league goals Barcelona lead 312-309 [9][10]. Barcelona earned their 106th win last season at Camp Nou, in the game that secured the league title [12].

Previous El Clásicos

Recent history favours Barcelona. Real Madrid beat Barcelona four times in a row in all competitions between 2023 and 2024; then the wind turned, and Barcelona won six of the first seven El Clásicos under Flick [4]. According to Al Jazeera's round-up, Barcelona have 6 wins and Real Madrid 4 in the last 10 El Clásicos [11].

Last season's game at Camp Nou was the one that brought Barcelona their 29th league title [2]. Tonight's game may not decide the season's direction, but it could set its tone: the return fixture is on 9 May 2027 at the Santiago Bernabéu [1].

Final word

Barcelona have the record, the form and home advantage; Real Madrid have Mourinho's unbeaten run against this opponent and a perfect October. The absentee lists, meanwhile, test both sides. The real question is this: can Flick's trimmed-down defence stop Mbappé for 90 minutes? The answer starts tonight at 23:00.
