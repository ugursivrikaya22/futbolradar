Fenerbahçe 3-2 Liverpool: Liverpool Carried the Ball, Set Pieces Wrote the Game

FutbolRadar 04 November 2026

32% possession, 1.70 xG, 0.71 xG from set pieces, and all three goals from set pieces or in the air: the numbers of the Kadıköy night.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Dear futbolradar.com readers, the result is clear: in matchday four of the UEFA Champions League league phase, Fenerbahçe beat Liverpool 3-2 at the Chobani Stadium (Şükrü Saracoğlu) [1][4]. Liverpool went ahead twice, Fenerbahçe answered twice and turned the game around in the 79th minute [1][3]. The game was refereed by France's Clément Turpin [1]. On the touchline were interim coach Dirk Kuyt on one side and Andoni Iraola on the other [1][5].

This is my reading: telling this win as the story of "a team that held on" would be unfair. Fenerbahçe did not hold on; they gave up the ball and moved the game onto set pieces and the air, playing where Liverpool are weakest.

Match summary: five goals, three different breaking points

Goals [1][3][4]:

25' Trey Nyoni (Liverpool), 0-1

29' Mason Greenwood (Fenerbahçe), 1-1 — free kick, deflected in off Jacquet

69' Alexander Isak (Liverpool), 1-2 — header from Gakpo's cross

73' Archie Brown (Fenerbahçe), 2-2 — after a short corner

79' Vedat Muriqi (Fenerbahçe), 3-2 — header from Brown's cross

It was 1-1 at half-time [1]. According to Hürriyet, Greenwood became the first player to score a free kick in the Champions League in a Fenerbahçe shirt [4]. Isak scored with his first touch after coming on; according to ESPN, Muriqi's goal was his first in the Champions League [3].

This is my reading: both of Liverpool's goals were products of individual quality; Fenerbahçe's three goals were the product of a plan.

32% possession, higher xG

The key numbers of the game [1][2]:

Possession: Fenerbahçe 32%, Liverpool 68%

Shots: Fenerbahçe 17, Liverpool 22

Shots on target: 6 – 6

Expected goals (xG): Fenerbahçe 1.70, Liverpool 1.57

Touches in the opposition box: Fenerbahçe 17, Liverpool 35

Big chances: Fenerbahçe 2, Liverpool 4

Passes: Fenerbahçe 260, Liverpool 529

Liverpool made twice as many passes, had twice as many touches in the box and took 12 corners to 4 [1]. Yet it was Fenerbahçe who came out ahead on expected goals [1][2].

This is my reading: this table is the cleanest proof that possession on its own has no value. Liverpool occupied the pitch, but the quality of the chances they created could not surpass that of an opponent who kept the ball a third of the time.

The real story: set pieces and aerial balls

Look inside the xG and the key to the game appears. According to BBC data, Fenerbahçe's xG from set pieces was 0.71, Liverpool's 0.45 [1]. In open play Liverpool were ahead: 1.12 to 0.98 [1]. Fenerbahçe also won the aerial battle 11 to 9 [1].

Look at the goals: Greenwood's was a free kick, Brown's came from a corner, and Muriqi's, as BBC describes it, came from a long throw headed back to Brown and Brown's cross [1][3][4]. All three goals were moves that started from a set piece or finished in the air.

This is my reading: Kuyt's Fenerbahçe accepted that they could not compete with Liverpool in open play and reduced the game to moments whose terms they could set themselves. That is not a defensive choice; it is an efficient one.

What brought down the three-from-three leaders?

Liverpool had started the league phase perfectly: they beat Atlético Madrid 2-1, LASK 6-3 away and Villarreal 4-3 [6][7][8]. Before the game Liverpool had 3 wins and 9 points, while Fenerbahçe took the field with a 0-2-1 record and 2 points [2]. With this result Fenerbahçe moved up to 5 points, Liverpool stayed on 9 [1].

In our preview we said of Liverpool, "they win, but they do not keep it tight" (our preview column). A team that conceded three to Villarreal and three to LASK conceded three again tonight [7][8][1].

Isak and Gakpo coming on in the 66th minute and producing a goal within four minutes showed Iraola's move was right; but four minutes after going ahead, Liverpool conceded again [1][3].

This is my reading: what brought Liverpool down was not bad luck, but the moments when the ball hung in the air in their own box. Iraola's side are superior with the ball, fragile without it and in the air; Fenerbahçe read exactly that line.

Closing

With this win Fenerbahçe took their first victory of the league phase [1][2]. And against Liverpool's 22 shots, Ederson and the defence held the shots on target to 6 [1]. For the team's form and squad this season, see our Fenerbahçe team page; for Liverpool's 6-3 away win at LASK, our match page.

This is my reading: the lesson learned at Kadıköy tonight is this: a team with 32% of the ball can get ahead of a giant on three from three if it picks the right moments. The question for Fenerbahçe now is whether this plan was a one-night affair; if the set-piece weapon is here to stay, this team's European story can be rewritten.
