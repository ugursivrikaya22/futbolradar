Galatasaray v Stuttgart: The Game Starts in the 46th Minute

FutbolRadar 03 November 2026

Galatasaray v Stuttgart this evening at 20:45 at RAMS Park: form, absentees, key players and the second-half test, in numbers.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Update · 03 November 2026 at 15:15

Transfer agenda section added: Galatasaray Development Director Fatih Demireli's statement on El Khannouss and Demirović (official), plus the summer offer for Demirović and the January claims (rumour, with sources).

This season Galatasaray have opened the scoring in all three of their Champions League games: in the 5th minute away at Sporting, the 28th against Barcelona and the 2nd at Lille [4][5]. Despite that, they are 23rd in the table on 3 points [3]. According to BBC's tournament data, the yellow-and-reds have dropped 6 points from winning positions; for Stuttgart that figure is 2 [3]. That is why the question for matchday four of the league phase, which kicks off at RAMS Park at 20:45 (Turkish time) this evening with referee Chris Kavanagh's whistle, is not "who scores first" but "who is still standing in the second half" [1][2].

For match details, line-ups and the live score, see our Galatasaray – Stuttgart match centre page.

Form: two different kinds of momentum

Galatasaray's last five competitive games [3][5][10][12]:

13 October: Galatasaray 1-4 Barcelona (UCL)

17 October: Gençlerbirliği 0-1 Galatasaray (Süper Lig)

21 October: Lille 2-4 Galatasaray (UCL)

26 October: Galatasaray 2-1 Fenerbahçe (Süper Lig)

30 October: Konyaspor 0-4 Galatasaray (Süper Lig)

In the league the run since the Trabzonspor defeat is four games, and the team are top on 25 points [1][11]. In Konya the goals came from Osimhen, Yunus Akgün, Batrakov and Leão from the penalty spot [10].

Stuttgart's last five competitive games [1][6][7][8]:

14 October: Slovan Bratislava 0-3 Stuttgart (UCL)

20 October: Stuttgart 1-1 Atlético Madrid (UCL)

24 October: Stuttgart 2-3 Mönchengladbach (Bundesliga)

28 October: Paderborn 0-1 Stuttgart (DFB-Pokal, Undav scoring in the 90th minute)

31 October: Leverkusen 2-4 Stuttgart (Bundesliga)

Sebastian Hoeneß's side are mid-table in the Bundesliga with 3 wins and 5 defeats from 8 games; in Europe, though, they are unbeaten [1]. The win at Leverkusen lifted them to 10th [9]. In the Gladbach game the Bundesliga Match Facts xGoals figure was 1.19 to 2.38 against them; at Leverkusen it was 3.23 to 1.77 in their favour [6][7].

This is my reading: Stuttgart's Bundesliga position is misleading; the version Galatasaray will face is the one that won at Leverkusen while producing more than 3 expected goals.

What the numbers say: Galatasaray's breaking-point minutes

Against Sporting, Galatasaray were 1-1 at half-time, conceded 2 in the second half and lost 3-1; against Barcelona it was also 1-1 at the break, and 3 goals came between the 50th and 65th minutes [4]. So of the 7 goals conceded in the first two games, 5 came after half-time [4]. At Lille the picture was reversed: it was 2-2 at half-time, and in the second half Torreira (52) and Osimhen (61) took the score to 4-2 [5].

The tournament totals show the same imbalance [3]:

Galatasaray: 21 shots, 10 on target, 6 goals, 9 conceded

Stuttgart: 41 shots, 16 on target, 7 goals, 2 conceded

Pass accuracy: Galatasaray 83.24%, Stuttgart 88.2%

Galatasaray are converting more than half of their shots on target; Stuttgart have attempted almost twice as many shots as Galatasaray. According to the club's preview note, Stuttgart have scored in each of their last 11 away games in the Champions League group/league phase [1].

This is my reading: Galatasaray's real opponent this evening is not Stuttgart's attacking volume but their own second-half management. After the Barcelona game Okan Buruk said "after 3-1 the game slipped out of our hands" [4]; Lille is the only proof that this gap can be closed.

Squad news and key players

Galatasaray have nobody injured or suspended; Kaan Ayhan, Kazımcan Karataş, Arda Ünyay, Eyüp Aydın and Can Armando Güner, who are not on the UEFA list, cannot play [2]. Stuttgart are without Angelo Stiller, Leo Sauer and Josha Vagnoman through injury [2]. Stiller also missed the Leverkusen game with an adductor injury [6].

NTV Spor's probable XIs [2]:

Galatasaray: Uğurcan, Singo, Davinson, Abdülkerim, Jakobs, Lemina, Torreira, Yunus, Batrakov, Barış Alper, Osimhen

Stuttgart: Seimen, Leweling, Hendriks, Chabot, Mittelstädt, Atakan (Karazor), Prömel, El Khannouss, Deniz (Undav), Führich, Demirović

Bundesliga.com predicts a 3-4-2-1 formation for Stuttgart [9].

Victor Osimhen, with 9 goals in 8 games, is Galatasaray's top scorer; he needs 2 goals to reach the 10 of Burak Yılmaz, the second-highest scorer in the club's Champions League history [1]. Aleksey Batrakov has scored against both Barcelona and Lille [4][5]. On the other side, Ermedin Demirović has reached 5 goals in 8 Bundesliga games; he scored 4 in his last two league games [7][6][9]. Deniz Undav assisted Demirović's second goal at Leverkusen and scored the goal that won the tie at Paderborn [6][8].

This is my reading: Stiller's absence leaves Stuttgart's midfield rhythm to the Karazor–Prömel pairing; that is exactly where Torreira and Lemina win the ball.

Table and history: what can be won, what can be lost?

In the league phase the top 8 go straight into the last 16, and 9th to 24th go into the play-offs [3]. Stuttgart are 6th on 7 points, Galatasaray 23rd on 3 [3]. Galatasaray's next Champions League game is at home again, against Aston Villa on 24 November [1].

The two teams are meeting for the first time in a competitive match [1][2]. In European competition Galatasaray have 7 wins, 13 draws and 13 defeats from 33 games against German teams; they have lost the last 4 [2]. Their last home win against German opposition was 1-0 against Eintracht Frankfurt on 4 November 1992; the 9 home games since then have brought 5 draws and 4 defeats [1][2]. Stuttgart's only Turkish opponents in UEFA competition were Fenerbahçe, and they lost that game 1-0 [2].

This is my reading: if a 34-year home run is to be broken this evening, the way to do it runs less through the first goal than through the 30 minutes after the 60th. If Galatasaray play a second half from in front the way they did at Lille, the top-24 maths gets easier; if they play it as at Sporting and Barcelona, the value of the first goal is wiped out again.

Transfer agenda: two Stuttgart players were on Galatasaray's list

Official: in an interview with the German magazine Kicker, Galatasaray Development Director Fatih Demireli confirmed that during the summer transfer window they were interested in Stuttgart's Bilal El Khannouss and Ermedin Demirović [13][14]. Demireli said they had good talks with El Khannouss, Stuttgart and his agents, and in the end signed Aleksey Batrakov; on Demirović he said: "In the end we consciously chose other solutions" [13][14].

Rumour (Fanatik, citing Sky Sport): in the summer Galatasaray made an offer for Demirović of €18 million guaranteed plus €2 million in bonuses, €20 million in total; the clubs could not agree and the player chose to stay at Stuttgart [16].

Rumour (beIN SPORTS, citing SportSport in the Bosnian press): Galatasaray are expected to knock on Stuttgart's door again in January for Demirović on similar terms, with a three-year contract and a net salary of €4 million a season; Stuttgart are said to want at least €35 million [15].

This is my reading: two names in Stuttgart's probable XI this evening were players Galatasaray put on the table in the summer. The January move is, for now, a claim by the press rather than the club; Demireli's words only concern the summer window. The impression Demirović leaves at RAMS Park could also decide how long this rumour lives.

Final word

Galatasaray have no problem scoring in this competition; 6 goals in three games, from 10 shots on target [3]. The problem is carrying the score. Stuttgart, on the other hand, have conceded only 2 goals in 3 games in Europe [3].

This is my reading: this evening's most important statistic will be written after half-time.
