Real Madrid Third: 12 out of 12 in Europe, but Atlético Have Got in Between in the League

FutbolRadar 05 November 2026

Real Madrid 3rd in the league, four from four in Europe: Atlético have got in between, Barça are 3 points ahead.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Dear futbolradar.com readers, on the morning of Thursday 5 November the La Liga table shows a three-coloured truth: Barcelona 30, Atlético Madrid 28, Real Madrid 27 points [1][7]. Last night in Athens we turned 2-1 down into a 3-2 win over AEK to make it 12 out of 12 in the Champions League [3][4]; in the league, though, we are still third. Since the 2-1 El Clásico win at Camp Nou, sealed by Mbappé's 88th-minute goal (our Clásico full-time column), the three-point gap has held; but Atlético have squeezed in between.

This is my reading: I am a Madridista; a perfect record in Europe is not enough to lift us off third in the league. "We are three points behind Barça" is not a consolation, it is a warning — because there are now two rivals, not one.

Third place: Atlético have got in between

As of matchweek 11, Barcelona are on 30 points from 11 games with 10 wins, 0 draws and 1 defeat; Atlético on 28 with 9-1-1; Real Madrid on 27 with 9-0-2 [1][7]. On goal difference Barcelona are +34 (46-12), Madrid +18 (28-10), Atlético +14 (22-8) [1]. So on goal difference we are still behind Barça but ahead of Atlético; in the table we are behind both. On 1 November we beat Racing 3-0 at El Sardinero: Mbappé from the penalty spot (11'), Bellingham (21') and a strike from outside the box by Diomande (36') wrote the score; Mourinho's side made it six straight wins in all competitions [2]. Five from five at home, 4 wins and 2 defeats in 6 away games: the AS table shows 15 home points and 12 away [1].

This is my reading: the Racing score produces morale, not position. Atlético's 28 points have turned the derby between the Bernabéu and the Metropolitano from a "neighbours' race" into a title triangle. Seasons in which you underestimate the neighbours end up costing you dearly in May.

Barça the goal machine, Madrid the control machine

Barcelona have scored 46 goals in 11 games; more than four a game [1]. In the same period Madrid have found 28 and conceded 10 [1][7]. Atlético have scored 22 and conceded only 8; in defensive efficiency they are the tightest of the three [1]. The two giants have yet to draw in the league: they either win or lose [1]. Flick's attacking tempo and Mourinho's control of the score sit side by side in the same table; but the table rewards tempo. For the current standings see the La Liga standings page; for the El Clásico background there is our Barcelona–Real Madrid page.

This is my reading: 46 to 28 cannot be read as "we are more solid". Solidity is not goals conceded; it is points lost. Two league defeats weigh more than comebacks in Athens — because in the league there is no extra time, only Sunday.

12 out of 12 in Athens

Wednesday 4 November, ALLWYN Arena: AEK 2-3 Real Madrid. Koita 12', Bellingham 32', Pilios 75', Mbappé 78', Valverde 90+2' [3][4]. A team that fell behind twice and won; four from four in the Champions League league phase, 12 points [3]. The club website wrote that Mbappé has scored in all four European games and that Valverde closed out the night with a strike from outside the box [3]. ESPN reported the same score and Valverde's stoppage-time winner [4].

This is my reading: in Europe this Madrid carries "comeback DNA"; in the league, the same DNA has not yet been written into the table. 12 out of 12 in Athens does not bring the summit; what brings the summit is moving from third to second in the league, and then to first.

The winter window is shut, and the JJ Gabriel claim

According to an AS report dated 2 November, Real Madrid are not planning any incoming moves in the winter transfer window; the board believes the squad is strong enough to emerge from "a year and a half of drought" [6]. An exit is on the table if a player wants it and Mourinho opens the door; for now it does not look likely [6].

Claim (BBC Sport, 4 November 2026): Real Madrid have reached a verbal agreement with 16-year-old Manchester United forward JJ Gabriel; there have been no talks between the clubs yet, and the signing is expected in January. Barcelona and PSG had also shown interest; the player has obtained a European Union passport [5].

This is my reading: it is hard to carry a "closed market" in winter and a claim about a 16-year-old in January in the same sentence. Without an official club announcement I do not count the Gabriel file as part of the squad plan; but BBC's named claim sits alongside Valdebebas's "sufficient squad" narrative. One is the language of the board, the other a bet on the future.

Closing

The summary is clear: 27 points / 3rd in the league, 12 out of 12 in Europe, 3-0 at Racing, 3-2 in Athens [1][2][3][7]. Barcelona are raining goals on 30; Atlético have got in between on 28; Madrid have to watch both above and beside them. The winter-is-closed narrative [6] and the Gabriel claim [5] collide in the same week. Matchweek 12 is at the door; the table's triangle will speak faster than the European record.

This is my reading: I love this team; love is not applauding third place. Turning the perfect record in Europe into a winning hand in the league triangle is Mourinho's real test. If the table does not improve, the nights in Athens will become memories, not a title.
