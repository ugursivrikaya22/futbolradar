Those Hands Beneath the Stone

FutbolRadar 02 August 2026

Aziz Yıldırım's return to the lectern after eight years is not simply a boardroom move at Fenerbahçe; it reads like the return of an old story carrying its weight, its age and its sense of belonging all at once.

As the wind off Kalamış beat against the windows of the Faruk Ilgaz facilities yesterday, a man who has spent his life defying the years was sitting in that hall. Grey in his hair, time pressing on his shoulders; but the look in his eyes was still that of the stubborn, hard-headed man of 1998.

Aziz Yıldırım is back at his own lectern after eight years.

Consider it. He stayed a full twenty years in the chair he took in February 1998, beating Vefa Küçük by a single vote. He rebuilt Şükrü Saracoğlu from top to bottom, had the Ülker Arena put up, built facility after facility, took the club to European semi-finals and carried six league titles into this community's museum. Then in 2018 he left. His departure was like the man himself; upright, hard, alone.

He stayed away for eight years. Those eight years were also the longest title drought in Fenerbahçe's history. A club that had gone without the league trophy since 2014 changed president after president; extraordinary general meetings became another word for exhaustion. And last June the community turned back to him. Seventeen thousand two hundred and forty-five votes. A record. The old chief had been called back.

Yesterday we saw what lay beneath that call.

The man who sprang from his seat while Orhan Demirel was speaking and thundered, "Are you guilty, Orhan? You are not guilty, why are you defending yourself?" was not talking about a reckoning but about a burden. "I didn't come here to catch thieves, I'm not a policeman," he said. "I came to make Fenerbahçe champions, and I will."

But that was not the sentence that buried the hall in silence.

His voice trembling for a moment, he said: "I expect nothing from Fenerbahçe, nothing at all. I grieve for my daughter, for my grandchildren, for the young ones. I came here for them, for them."

The whole story is hidden inside that sentence.

Is there any other explanation for a man of seventy-three, with no office left to win and no title left to lose, throwing himself back into the middle of this fight? "We put our hands beneath the stone," he said. "The stone is making us bleed, our hands are covered in blood, but we are not objecting. Because Fenerbahçe has no other chance."

Those hands... hands that carried the stone and the brick of this club for twenty years. After an eight-year gap they are going back under the same stone. They are bleeding, and their owner says, "we are not objecting."

You understand him and you grieve for him at once. Because behind that hard man shouting at the lectern stands a grandfather wounded by the language aimed at his family on social media, a man whose voice changes when his daughter and grandchildren are mentioned. Beneath the armour of the chief is a person who gets tired, like everyone else.

Perhaps that is the cruellest side of football. Men give their youth to this devotion; then one day it comes back and asks for their old age too. And those men say "there is no other chance" and come back.

Aziz Yıldırım made a promise for this season: one year, one title, then farewell. This community will see on the pitch, on the green grass, whether that promise can be kept. But what was visible in that old hall yesterday was something else: this man is there not even because he loves it — but because he knows nothing other than loving it.

Those hands beneath the stone are bleeding again.

And still, he is not objecting.
