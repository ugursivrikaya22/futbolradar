Batrakov's Three Minutes: Galatasaray Pass the First-Half Test, the Real Question Comes on 46

FutbolRadar 03 November 2026

2-0 midway through the first half: Batrakov's two goals in three minutes, Stuttgart's bad luck with the post and Galatasaray's 'losing from in front' problem, in numbers.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

*This column was written during the first half of the game, at around the 23rd minute, with the score at 2-0, at 21:10 (Turkish time). A full-time assessment will be published separately.*

This morning in our preview column we said "the game starts in the 46th minute". The first quarter of an hour at RAMS Park both confirmed and tested that thesis. Aleksey Batrakov struck into the top right corner from the middle of the box in the 12th minute, and from the right-hand side of the area from Davinson Sánchez's pass in the 15th: Galatasaray lead 2-0 [1]. Two goals in three minutes. For a Galatasaray side that were 23rd on 3 points before the game, that means breathing room in the league phase; BBC's live table currently shows the yellow-and-reds in 14th [1]. For the live score, line-ups and statistics, see our Galatasaray – Stuttgart match page.

The first 20 minutes: Stuttgart hit the post, Galatasaray the net

The scoreboard is one-sided; the game itself is not so much. According to BBC Sport's live data [1]:

12' Batrakov (1-0) 15' Batrakov, assisted by Davinson Sánchez (2-0) Shots on target: Galatasaray 2, Stuttgart 0 (as of the 15th minute) xG: Galatasaray 0.41 – Stuttgart 0.46 (at the time of writing) Possession: Galatasaray 58.8% (it was 64.6% in the 15th minute)

Stuttgart's threat is real: in the 8th minute Jamie Leweling hit the left post from very close range from Mittelstädt's cross; in the 14th Undav's long-range shot went wide [1]. Atakan Karazor was booked as early as the 2nd minute after a hard foul [1]. The referee is Chris Kavanagh [1][3].

This is my reading: Galatasaray turned two shots on target into two goals; that efficiency is not a sustainable game plan, it is an advance. Karazor's early booking, meanwhile, is a burden that will rein in Stuttgart's physicality in midfield for 70 minutes.

Batrakov and the summer file

It is hard to read these goals separately from the transfer file. Official: Galatasaray Development Director Fatih Demireli told Kicker that over the summer they had been interested in Stuttgart's Bilal El Khannouss and Ermedin Demirović (NTV Spor, Sözcü) [7][8]. El Khannouss is on the pitch this evening as Stuttgart's number ten; in Galatasaray's number-ten role is Batrakov [1][2]. AA had reported that the 21-year-old Russian scored 3 goals in his last 5 competitive games before this one [3].

Rumour (beIN SPORTS): it is claimed that Galatasaray could come back in for Demirović in January [9]. Rumour (Fanatik): it was reported that Galatasaray made a €20 million offer for Demirović in the summer, but the transfer did not happen [10].

This is my reading: with one of the two names on the table in the summer playing against them this evening, the player Galatasaray brought in for that area answering with two goals vindicated the club's squad planning in the first half. Demirović, though, is still on the pitch and still a danger.

The numbers' warning: Germany and leading positions

History is not on Galatasaray's side. According to data reported by T24, the yellow-and-reds have lost 6 and drawn 1 of their last 7 European games against German teams; their last win was 3-2 against Schalke in Gelsenkirchen on 12 March 2013 [4][5]. The last 4 games were all defeats [4]. Galatasaray have also failed to win any of their last 9 home European games against German teams [4].

Stuttgart are not opponents who give up easily either. In the league phase they beat Viking 3-1 and Slovan Bratislava 3-0 and drew 1-1 with Atlético Madrid; they scored 7 and conceded only 2 [3]. Demirović shares top spot in the competition's scoring charts with 4 goals in the first 3 games [2][3]. According to Bundesliga.com, Stuttgart have scored in each of their last 11 away games in the Champions League group/league phase [2].

And most importantly: according to BBC's tournament data, Galatasaray have dropped 6 points from winning positions this season [1].

This is my reading: 2-0 may be Galatasaray's most comfortable interim score this season, but "losing from in front" is this team's main problem in the Champions League. Stuttgart's habit of scoring on every away trip could make the second half far tenser than 2-0 promises.

Absentees and the bench: the second half's trump cards

Stuttgart are without Angelo Stiller (groin), Leo Sauer and Josha Vagnoman through injury [3][6]; Zagadou and Justin Diehl are also on the injury list [6]. Galatasaray have nobody injured or suspended; Kaan Ayhan, Kazımcan Karataş and Arda Ünyay cannot play because they are not in the UEFA squad [3].

On the Galatasaray bench, Gündoğan, Sané, Rafael Leão and Gabriel Sara are waiting [1]. Osimhen came into the game with 9 goals in 8 Süper Lig games (10 in 10 in all competitions) [3].

Things were heated off the pitch too: the day before the game, Stuttgart's official X account posted a photo of the loudspeakers at RAMS Park with a "thinking" emoji, then deleted the post after the backlash and apologised in Turkish; Galatasaray replied "Thank you" [11][12].

This is my reading: Stuttgart's 3-4-2-1 is missing Stiller's distribution at the back. Sané and Leão on the Galatasaray bench are ideal cards for the counter as Stuttgart open up; the timing of the Galatasaray staff's substitutions will be this evening's real test.

Conclusion: the score is doing the talking, not the loudspeakers

Midway through the first half, Galatasaray have answered the first half of the question we asked before the game: going ahead is not this team's problem. From the 46th minute, though, the 6 points dropped from winning positions and the 13-year German drought will be tested at the same time. Our full-time column will be on these pages at around 23:15.
