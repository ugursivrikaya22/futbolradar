The Moment Time Stood Still at Ninety Plus Two

FutbolRadar 07 November 2026

Those few seconds at ninety plus two when everyone held their breath.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Yira Sor's goal silenced RAMS Park for a moment. Then the whistle blew and the noise of the stands came back.

When Barış Alper's header went in on eighty minutes, the game looked over. The tension of slipping from 3-0 to 3-2 had lifted, and the stands had finally sat back.

Then came ninety plus two. A ball deflecting off the defence, Yira Sor in the box, and the net: 4-3.

At that moment something strange happened at RAMS Park, and the sound cut out. No whistling, no shouting. Thousands of people held their breath at once, all asking the same thing: will another one come? No longer were minutes being counted, but seconds.

That silence is football's most honest moment. In those seconds a fan thinks neither about the league table nor about the winning run. Their eyes are only on the pitch: on Uğurcan, on Sanchez, on any foot that might clear the ball.

And Halil Umut Meler blew his whistle.

What burst out was relief more than joy. A sound that came from every chest at once, laughing a little at itself. Nobody asked why we had been so scared when we were 3-0 up, because love does not do the maths.

Galatasaray went into the international break top on 28 points and equalled the 41-game unbeaten home record. But what will stay in the mind from this evening is not the numbers; it is those few seconds of silence. Sometimes you realise you have won a game while waiting for the goal that never comes.
