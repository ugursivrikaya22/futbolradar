Three Giants, Three Games, Three 2-1s: La Liga's Title Triangle

FutbolRadar 10 November 2026

Barcelona 33, Real Madrid 30, Atlético 28: the three giants have each beaten another 2-1, and the title will go to whoever breaks the cycle first.

Rock, paper, scissors: for now everyone has beaten someone and lost to someone. The trophy will go to whoever breaks the cycle first.

Three Giants, Three Games, Three 2-1s

La Liga goes into the international break with a summit playing rock, paper, scissors. Atlético beat Real Madrid 2-1 at the Metropolitano on 20 September [6]. Real Madrid beat Barcelona 2-1 at Camp Nou on 25 October [7]. And Barcelona beat Atlético 2-1 at the Metropolitano on 8 November [2][3]. Each of the big three has beaten one of the others and lost to one. All three games finished with the same score.

After 12 weeks the table reads: Barcelona 33, Real Madrid 30, Atlético Madrid 28 points [1]. Fourth-placed Betis wait at the door on 26 points, with Real Sociedad on 22 [1]. There are 26 games and 78 points still to play for. So nothing is decided. But the character of the race is clear: three different kinds of football, one trophy. For the full table, see our La Liga standings page.

Barcelona: Four Goals a Game, One Wound

Flick's side have won 11 of their 12 games. They have scored 48 and conceded 13 [1][2]. That is four goals a game and a goal difference of +35. In the scoring charts Raphinha leads with 15 goals and Lamine Yamal is third with 10. Yamal also tops the assists list with seven [8].

The game at the Metropolitano showed why this team is top. Giuliano Simeone put Atlético ahead in the 10th minute. Barcelona did not fall apart. Fermín López equalised in the 45th minute and turned the game around in the 66th from Yamal's through ball [2][3]. Fermín is 18th in our player market values list at €110 million. This season he is earning that figure.

Barça's only wound is their only defeat: El Clásico. That 2-1 did not just cost three points; it left a head-to-head debt that will be carried until May. Barcelona are top, but their account with their biggest rival is still open.

Real Madrid: Mourinho's Quiet Pursuit

Since the Clásico, Real Madrid have not raised their voice; they have simply kept winning. On 7 November they beat Valencia 3-0 at Mestalla. One of the goals was Arda Güler's. Arda sent Bellingham's cut-back into the net first time in the 36th minute, then set up Mbappé's goal in the 75th [4][5]. He stayed on the pitch for 84 minutes [4].

According to the club's match report, that was an eighth straight win in all competitions, and Mbappé scored in a seventh consecutive game [5]. In the league they are on a five-match winning run [4]; the table shows 10 wins, 2 defeats and goals of 31-10 [1]. A week ago Madrid were third; now they are second again. One of the two defeats was the derby at the Metropolitano. Huijsen was sent off early in the second half, and ten-man Madrid lost 2-1 despite Rüdiger's 89th-minute goal [6].

Madrid's biggest trump card is the head-to-head record. In Spain, when teams finish level on points, the games between them are looked at first. Madrid, who came away from Camp Nou with a 2-1 win, host Barcelona at the Bernabéu on 9 May [7][9]. For Barça to win the head-to-head, they need to win there by at least two goals. A one-goal win would leave the head-to-head level and send it to overall goal difference. That is why, for Madrid, the three-point gap is just one game.

Atlético: A Solid Wall, a Blunt Edge

Simeone's side have conceded only 10 goals in 12 games. Together with Real Madrid, that is the best defence among the top three [1]. The problem is at the other end. Atlético have scored 23 goals, fewer than half of Barcelona's tally [1].

The Barça game summed it up. Julián Álvarez was one-on-one with the goalkeeper in the sixth minute and did not score. In the 55th minute another clear chance went over the bar. Marc Pubill also hit the crossbar [3]. A team that cannot turn these chances into goals on the big nights always stays one step behind in a title race.

But writing Atlético out of the race would be a mistake. They won the derby, and for now they lead Madrid on head-to-head [6]. The return game at the Bernabéu on 4 April could be one of the decisive nights of the race [9]. And then there is Betis coming up behind: 26 points, two behind Atlético [1].

Fixtures: The Key Bends After the Break

The league returns on 21 November. Real Madrid host Celta on Saturday evening (21:00 local time) [11]. Barcelona welcome Villarreal to Spotify Camp Nou the next day (14:00 local time) [10]. Atlético travel to Getafe on the same day [2][14].

December is intertwined with Europe. Real Madrid's calendar has PSV and Arsenal in the Champions League, and Athletic Club and Osasuna in the league [12]. Atlético will face fourth-placed Betis [14]. Barcelona play Real Sociedad on 20 December and begin the new year with the Espanyol derby on 3 January [13].

The real reckoning comes in spring. The Real Madrid–Atlético derby is in week 30, on 4 April at the Bernabéu. Real Madrid–Barcelona is in week 35, on 9 May, also at the Bernabéu [9]. The Atlético–Barcelona return game will be played in Barcelona. So two of the three return games in the triangle are at the Bernabéu.

Who Are the Favourites?

For today, I make Barcelona the favourites. 33 points, 48 goals, two players in the top three of the scoring charts and a team that can turn games around from behind [1][8]. That picture will not be easily broken.

But Real Madrid hold two trump cards: an eight-game winning run and the head-to-head [5][7]. Three points is one game. The night at the Bernabéu on 9 May could decide the title directly.

For Atlético the recipe is simple but hard: find goals. Their defence is a title-winning defence. Their attack, as in the Barça game, wastes chances on the big nights [3]. If they solve that, the triangle will live until the end.

There are six months until May. Rock, paper, scissors: for now everyone has beaten someone and lost to someone. The trophy will go to whoever breaks the cycle first.
