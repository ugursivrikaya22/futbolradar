More Than a Left Foot: Remembering Cevad Prekazi

FutbolRadar 09 November 2026

"When I die, this shirt and the Partizan shirt will be with me in my coffin."

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

The night the news came

On Sunday night the news arrived, and for a long time nobody knew what to say. Cevad Prekazi passed away at home at the age of 69. He had been battling health problems in his final years. [1] [3]

We will always remember him the same way: calm over the ball, eyes on the goal, the man who made a whole generation hold its breath.

There are no numbers in this piece. Those who loved Prekazi remember him not by figures but by the feeling he left behind. And that feeling was much more than a left foot.

Two homelands, one heart

Talking about the land where he was born, Prekazi used to call it "the most beautiful country in the world". He would say that there, equality mattered more than wealth. Then came the war. In one interview he said: "With the war, a piece of my heart died." [6]

The rest of his heart he left to us. He came from another country and made this city, this crest and these people his home.

Years later, recalling that unforgettable goal in Europe, he said a single sentence: "That goal was for Türkiye." In that moment, he said, football came second, third, fourth. That was the greatest gift someone from far away could give a country: belonging. [7]

A last wish wrapped in a shirt

Writing this piece, one sentence stopped me more than any other. In 2016 he said: "When I die, this shirt and the Partizan shirt will be with me in my coffin." [4]

What someone wants to take on their final journey tells you most about them. Prekazi asked for no trophy and no medal. He asked for two shirts: one from the land where he was born, one from the city he gave his heart to.

Only a month ago he met the fans and was moved looking at old photographs. "There was never anything bad between the fans and me," he said. He praised the new stadiums, but would add with a sigh: "Now I miss the mud of Ali Sami Yen." [8] [6]

He spoke not as a star, a status he had long left behind, but as an ordinary man full of longing. Perhaps that is what made him so loved.

Two cities, the same sorrow

After the news, two clubs mourned on the same night. Galatasaray remembered him as someone who left a mark on memories with his football and his character, and closed its farewell message with one sentence: "We will not forget you, Prekazi." [1]

The club wrote one more sentence, perhaps the truest of all: "The name of Prekazi, who made a generation into Galatasaray fans, will live forever in hearts that beat yellow and red." [2]

From Belgrade, Partizan spoke too. For him Partizan was a "second home"; he went often and followed their games. Their farewell was short and warm: "Preki, thank you for everything, rest in peace." The Turkish Football Federation also announced that it had learned of his death with deep sorrow. [2] [5]

Two shirts, two cities, two communities. All his life Prekazi was a bridge between them. Even as he left, he brought them together in the same grief.

Rest in peace, Preki

Some people play for a club and leave. Others become part of that club's childhood. Prekazi was one of the latter: one of those names fathers tell their children about, their voices trembling as they speak.

As we wrote these lines, the funeral arrangements had not yet been announced. But one thing we know: he wanted to make his final journey with his two shirts. Our wish is that he is sent off exactly as he wanted.

We offer our condolences to his family, his loved ones and everyone who carries his name in their heart. In a corner of that muddy pitch you missed, there is still a stand waiting for you. Rest in peace, Preki. Thank you for everything.
