The Poetry of the Left Foot Falls Silent: Farewell, Cevad Prekazi

FutbolRadar 08 November 2026

He scored 40 goals in 185 games for Galatasaray between 1985 and 1991, and his 1989 free kick carried the club to a European Cup semi-final.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

The night the news came

On Sunday night, between the match highlights and the league tables, the news arrived: Cevad Prekazi has died. The Serbian press reported that Galatasaray's unforgettable left foot passed away at home. [1] [2]

With that news, a generation's childhood moved a little further away. For those who grew up beside the radio, in front of old tube televisions and on the concrete steps of Ali Sami Yen, Prekazi was more than a footballer.

He was the man who could turn even a dead ball into a story. And we all know that story by heart.

Cologne, 15 March 1989: one strike, one piece of history

European Cup quarter-final. Because of the incidents in the Neuchâtel Xamax match, Galatasaray had to play the return leg against Monaco not at home but at the Müngersdorfer Stadium in Cologne. A home side far from home, a handful of hope in a foreign city. [4]

In the 51st minute the ball sat on the ground roughly 35 metres from goal. Nobody expected a goal from there, except Prekazi. The strike from his left foot came to rest in the Monaco net, the match ended 1-1 and Galatasaray reached the semi-finals of Europe's biggest competition. [4] [6]

Years later, talking about that goal, he would say he never managed a strike like it again. Perhaps some moments only happen once. They are not repeated, only remembered and passed down from generation to generation. [5]

From Mitrovica to Ali Sami Yen

He was born in 1957 in the Kosovan city of Mitrovica. He came through at Partizan, passed through Hajduk Split and joined Galatasaray in 1985. [7]

185 games and 40 goals in six seasons. The numbers may look modest; but behind them stand back-to-back league titles in 1986-87 and 1987-88, and a team that pushed open a door in Europe. [3] [7]

After Galatasaray he played for Altay and Bakırköyspor before finishing his career back home. He held Turkish citizenship and for years taught football to children in Partizan's academy. He never fully left this country. [7]

More than a name

Some footballers leave statistics, others leave sentences. Prekazi belonged to the second group. Whenever a free kick is won from distance, the first name that comes to mind for Galatasaray fans, more than thirty years on, is still his.

His name even became the title of a book: Onur Bayrakçeken's "Prekazi Shoots, It's a Goal!". It is not easy for a footballer from another country to settle so deeply into a club's language.

Prekazi did not do it with his left foot alone. He became the face of the years when Galatasaray began to stand up to Europe; some of the courage behind the great nights that followed was born of that strike in Cologne.

Farewell, poet of the left foot

Tonight many Galatasaray fans will open those old images again. They will watch the man standing over the ball, the short run-up, the strike, and then the cries of joy blending into the Cologne night.

Cevad Prekazi is no longer with us. But his signature is on Galatasaray's first great act of courage in Europe, and that signature will neither fade with time nor be forgotten.

We offer our condolences to his family, his loved ones and the whole Galatasaray family. Rest in light, Prekazi. Thank you, poet of the left foot.
