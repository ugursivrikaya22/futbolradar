It's Not About Salah: Can Reis's System Carry Trabzonspor to the Title?

FutbolRadar 11 November 2026

The six-match run is Reis's work, not Salah's. The Freiburg night showed the system's one weak spot: no plan B against teams that sit in, and Onuachu outside the game.

Salah can be a team's best player; he cannot be its only idea.

FutbolRadar

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Two things happened at once in August

Two things happened at once in Trabzon in August. When Mohamed Salah landed, the city gave him a welcome nobody had seen before, from the airport to the stadium [11], and in the same days the team was falling apart on the pitch. Fatih Tekke resigned after a defeat, the board first refused it, then the two sides parted ways [12]. So the club that had just signed one of the biggest stars in the world on a free spent the opening weeks of the season without a head coach. Today that team has won six league matches in a row, sits second on 25 points, three behind the leader [1][3]. The first name that comes to mind to explain that table is Salah. Not wrong, but incomplete.

Salah's numbers speak for themselves: 9 goals and 3 assists in eleven rounds [8]. A 34-year-old who arrived as a free agent [10] running at the top of the Süper Lig scoring chart is a story in itself. In Alanya, the goal that settled the match in the 34th minute, from Onuachu's pass, was his [2]. But Salah arrived at the start of August, he was the same Salah in September, and in those days the team was losing its coach. The six straight wins do not start on the day Salah arrived; they start on the day Thomas Reis did. That is the difference.

Reis's quiet run

When Reis signed on 17 September, everyone in Trabzon knew what was expected: at the very least, put out the fire. He came from Ludogorets, knew Turkey from his time at Samsunspor, and signed a modest two-year deal [9]. He was not a name that set the place alight. He won his first five league matches and said that hearing about the run was nice, but that he saw himself as only one part of the team and that the real aim was to win the next matches and keep it going [6]. The sixth win came in Alanya: Pina in the 17th minute, Salah in the 34th [1]. This team now finishes matches in the first half and manages them in the second. For a month we have watched a Trabzonspor that wins without sending anyone into a panic. That is what the Trabzon stands have missed for years: not a wild night, but a calm run.

So what is this order built on? After the Gaziantep match Reis himself said the team had started slowly and sluggishly, and that a higher press and a shape closer to their own style turned the game in the second half [6]. That sentence deserves underlining: even in the middle of the run, the coach admits the team has not yet played a full match the way he wants. When a team keeps winning, opponents do one of two things: they defend deeper or they close up earlier. The match that showed Trabzonspor do not yet have an answer to that was played five days later at Papara Park.

The Freiburg night: not bad luck, a missing plan B

The Freiburg match was the most instructive ninety minutes of the season. Trabzonspor had 63% of the ball, completed 511 of 574 passes, delivered 31 crosses and took 15 shots, only two of them on target; their expected goals were 1.67 to Freiburg's 0.99 [4]. The result: 0-1. In the 14th minute Onana kept out a header from a corner and Kübler put the rebound in, and that was the match [5]. The first defeat after seven wins out of seven under Reis was also the first match without a goal in his tenure [4]. The anatomy is this: against an opponent that sits in, you control the whole pitch, but nobody finishes the job in the box. That night the word in the stands was that someone had put the evil eye on them. No. Not the evil eye; a missing plan B.

Because this team's goals are unevenly shared. While Salah flies with 9, Paul Onuachu has 2 in nine matches [8]. A striker who scored 7 in his first nine games on loan in 2023-24 being stuck on two this season is not a coincidence [8]; Reis's game feeds the wings and the lanes Salah cuts into, and the tall striker waiting in the middle is left isolated in that game. Onuachu's pass to Salah in Alanya is the clearest summary of his role in this team: not the one who scores, but the one who sets it up [1].

The interesting part is that the coach has made the diagnosis himself. When criticism turned on Onuachu after the Freiburg match, Reis's answer was clear: if you have a tall striker who wins headers and can hold the ball, you should build the play around him; Trabzonspor did not give him the balls he wanted and failed to use the advantages he offered [7]. So the problem is not the player but the road that leads to him. The same report says Reis will sit down with Onuachu privately to restore his confidence in him [7]. The first thing to think about in Trabzon during the international break is not a transfer; it is how to bring the striker they already have into this game.

The real rival: Trabzonspor's own past

The second thing is patience. Look at the table: Galatasaray 28, Trabzonspor 25, Amedspor 23, Beşiktaş 22; Fenerbahçe sixth on 19 [3]. With a third of the league gone, the race already looks squeezed between three or four teams, and Trabzonspor are in it. The leader is three points ahead, a single match. At this point Trabzonspor's biggest rival is not Galatasaray but their own past. How many times in recent years has this club climbed to the top in autumn and fallen apart when winter came? That is exactly why Reis's emphasis on keeping it going matters. The coach talks as if he does not believe in his own run; thank goodness he doesn't.

The international break is an opportunity for Trabzonspor. The team will not play for two weeks and hosts Eyüpspor on 22 November [8]. Matches that look easy on paper will be the real test of this season until the problem of the deep-sitting opponent is solved. Reis has two weeks: to find a second attacking idea against teams that close up, and to pull Onuachu, in his own words, into the centre of the game. Solve those two and Trabzonspor stay in the title race to the end. Fail, and the Freiburg night repeats itself; first in Europe, then in the league.

As for Salah: on the day he arrived in Trabzon he said he had come to win trophies [11]. He is keeping his word; nine goals prove it. But Salah can be a team's best player; he cannot be its only idea. What decides Trabzonspor's season will not be how many Salah scores, but who scores when he doesn't. Reis knows that. Whether there is a celebration in Trabzon in May will be decided by the answer found in these two quiet weeks.
