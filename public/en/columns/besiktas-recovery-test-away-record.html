Beşiktaş's Recovery Test: A Fortress at Tüpraş, Guests on the Road

FutbolRadar 04 November 2026

9 wins in 10 competitive games at Tüpraş, 4 points from 5 away league games: Beşiktaş's problem is not the squad but an identity that gets lost on the road.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Dear futbolradar.com readers, on the evening of 22 October Beşiktaş drew 2-2 with Crystal Palace in the UEFA Europa League league phase [1][2]. That same night Trabzonspor beat Hearts 2-0 in the Conference League [3][4]. The screens were on Beşiktaş's side too: in the 22 October Total ratings ranking reported by Habertürk, the Beşiktaş-Crystal Palace game on TRT 1 came first and Trabzonspor-Hearts second [5]. Interest is at its peak. But where is the team? This column's claim is this: Beşiktaş's problem is not squad quality, but an identity that gets lost once they leave their stadium.

The Palace night: control lost before going down to 10

According to BBC Sport, the game went as follows [1]:

45+2' Ndidi: 1-0 (assist Salih Özcan)

51' Pino: 1-1

55' Mateta: 1-2

73' Olaitan: 2-2

77' Ümit Akdağ: straight red card

BBC and ESPN put the red card in the 77th minute [1][2]; some Turkish press records give the 78th. According to ESPN, the card came after a challenge on Jorgen Strand Larsen [2]. Beşiktaş started this game with nine changes after the weekend defeat to Trabzonspor [1]. The BBC writes that Palace deserved the win more on the balance of play; Mateta's 6th-minute goal had also been ruled out for offside by VAR [1]. In the closing spell with ten men, no whistle came when Kamada wanted a penalty [1][2]. There were 38,622 spectators at Tüpraş [1].

This is my reading: going down to 10 men is not the story of this game but a footnote. The real break came in the four minutes between 51 and 55; the team that led at half-time handed the advantage to their opponents in four minutes.

Two different Beşiktaş: 15 from 15 at home, 4 from 15 away

According to a round-up sourced from Milliyet and published on Sporx [6]:

Süper Lig at home: 5 games, 5 wins

Süper Lig away: 5 games, 1 win, 1 draw, 3 defeats, 4 points

Away goals: 6 scored, 9 conceded

All competitive games at Tüpraş: 10 games, 9 wins, 1 draw, 25 scored, 6 conceded

According to the same source, the 4 points from the first five away games are the lowest start since 2022-23 [6]. That single draw at Tüpraş was the Palace game [6]. In other words, Beşiktaş don't lose at home; the points melt away outside the stadium.

The latest example is Kasımpaşa: 2-0 up through Trossard (28') and Vlahovic (47'), Beşiktaş had to settle for 2-2 after goals from Winck (60') and Rafferty (87') [7]. Italiano's words sum up the situation: "We can't show the quality and concentration we have at home when we play away, and we pay a very heavy price for it." [8]

This is my reading: what Beşiktaş need to "fix" is not the whole team but their second halves away from home. The second half at Palace, entered with a lead, and the 2-0 scoreline at Kasımpaşa slipped away in the same way.

The table and the bench: the real ground for the Italiano debate

According to the matchweek 10 table on the club's official website, Beşiktaş are 4th on 19 points with 6 wins, 1 draw and 3 defeats from 10 games; leaders Galatasaray have 25 points [9]. Trabzonspor are 3rd on 22 points [9] and beat Beşiktaş 2-0 in matchweek 8 of the league on 19 October [6].

The Sporx round-up writes that Italiano's squad choices and the timing of his changes in away games have been criticised: in the Trabzonspor game, after the gap grew to 2, the first changes came in the 76th minute; at Kasımpaşa, Olaitan was taken off in the 63rd minute and Murillo and Trossard in the 83rd [6].

This is my reading: the debate about the coach should be framed not by the question "should Italiano go?" but by "how do you protect a lead away from home?" Questioning wholesale a coach whose home record is 15 from 15 is looking for the problem in the wrong place.

Squad and transfers: the gap in midfield, the January rumours

According to Fotospor, Orkun Kökçü is not fully fit and will most likely not play away to Celtic; his alternative, Fabio Miretti, is not expected back for at least a month [10]. According to Sporx, Beşiktaş have won only 1 of the 4 games without Orkun [6].

There are two claims on the transfer front, and neither has been made official:

Claim (Fotospor): Beşiktaş are considering paying up to 10 million euros for Newcastle's Joe Willock in the winter window; if that fails, they will turn to CSKA Moscow's Matvey Kislyak [11].

Claim (Fanatik): The board are interested in West Ham's Greek centre-back Konstantinos Mavropanos, but the English club are not keen to sell the player in January [12].

This is my reading: it is no coincidence that both rumours target central midfield and the heart of the defence. Those two areas are exactly where the shortfall shows in a team that cannot hold a lead away from home.

Closing

In their next Europa League game, Beşiktaş travel to Celtic [10]. That will be the first test of whether the gulf between home and away is closing. For the team's current position, see the Beşiktaş standings and Beşiktaş team page; for their rivals' table, see Trabzonspor standings.

This is my reading: the top of the ratings list shows that interest in Beşiktaş is not over; a return to the title race, though, depends on being the same team once they walk out of Tüpraş's gates.
