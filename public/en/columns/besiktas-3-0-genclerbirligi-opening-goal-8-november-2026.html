Beşiktaş 3-0 Gençlerbirliği: The Opening Goal Was Not the Red Card

futbolradar 08 November 2026

Vlahovic scored at 20 and 45, Poku at 85; Gençlerbirliği went down to ten men in the 22nd minute.

Share this column
fShare on Facebook XShare on X

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Beşiktaş beat Gençlerbirliği 3-0 on the evening of 8 November at Tüpraş Stadium. Vlahovic scored in the 20th and 45th minutes, and Poku scored in the 85th. For the visitors, Metehan Baltacı left the pitch in the 22nd minute after a second yellow card. The sides went in at 2-0. That is the official record of the night. [1]

This take: Dear Beşiktaş supporter, sometimes the most honest way to enjoy a win is to tell it without inflating it. Let us neither shrink this night into "the opponent went down to ten, that is the whole story" nor hide every question behind three goals. Between the two, there is a place where we can be glad with a clear conscience.

The two minutes between 20 and 22

Vlahovic's opening goal arrived in the 20th minute; Metehan's red card came at 22. So Beşiktaş went ahead while the opponent still had eleven players. The second goal, in the 45th minute, came from Vlahovic again. [1]

This take: The order of those two minutes matters. If we start the story of the match only from the red card, we tell Beşiktaş's opening goal incompletely. We also cannot judge the football after the card as if it had been played on equal terms. Giving the win its due is not the same as ignoring that the opponent was reduced.

This take: The first thing I put in my pocket from this night is this: Beşiktaş did not wait for the opponent to be reduced before taking the lead. No larger sentence is needed to defend the supporters' joy.

Five changes, and Orkun back in the starting eleven

Vincenzo Italiano made five changes from the starting eleven of the Europa League match lost 3-2 away at Celtic. Rıdvan Yılmaz, Ndidi, Orkun Kökçü, İlhan Fakılı and Vlahovic came into the starting side. In the shape DHA reported, Salih Özcan, Ndidi and Orkun made up the midfield; İlhan was on the right of the attack, Trossard on the left, and Vlahovic up front. [2]

Orkun returned to the starting eleven after a five-match gap. He had come on later in the Celtic match; against Gençlerbirliği he was in the starting side and gave his place to Olaitan in the 70th minute. [1][2]

This take: I do not want to load Orkun's return with the meaning "he changed the whole setup" straight away. The teamsheet alone is not enough to say that. I can still understand the familiar feeling that comes from seeing his name in the starting eleven again from the stand. Sometimes a person is glad to have a footballer back before the team.

The answer at 85 from the player who came on at 77

On the official match timeline, Poku replaced İlhan in the 77th minute and scored the third goal in the 85th. A player who was not in the starting side also contributed to the score. [1]

This take: I will not turn these eight minutes into a "perfect substitution" story. It is easy to tell the purpose of a player's entrance and the making of the goal as if they were the same thing; proving it is something else. What we know is plainer: Poku came on and scored. If we are going to be glad for him, this is the solid ground.

This take: As supporters we carry an expectation when we look at the bench too. We do not only want the clock to run; we want someone who comes on to add something to the match. That is why Poku's share of the night feels a little more valuable to me than the final digit of the score.

After the previous draw, this time without conceding

In the previous league match Beşiktaş drew 2-2 away at Kasımpaşa. They finished the Gençlerbirliği match with three goals scored and none conceded. [1]

This take: The difference between the two results is welcome; let us not jump from there to the verdict "the defensive problem is solved". A match in which the opponent was reduced in the 22nd minute does not, by itself, answer every question about the defence. The value of this evening is not that it hands out guarantees belonging to tomorrow; it is that it puts a won, completed job in front of us.

This take: If you, like me, stay in front of the screen for a few more minutes after the final whistle, you know this feeling: you do not actually want to have solved everything. Sometimes you only want to see that your team won and close the evening a little lighter. Beşiktaş gave us that tonight. Let us live the joy of three goals; let us also carry the questions we left open into tomorrow, honestly.

— futbolradar
