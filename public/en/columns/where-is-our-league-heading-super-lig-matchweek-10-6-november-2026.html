Where Is Our League Heading? The Summit Is Wider Than the Big Three, and the Relegation Zone Is Not Quiet

FutbolRadar 06 November 2026

The summit has widened and the relegation zone is not quiet: where is the soul of our league?

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Dear futbolradar.com readers, after matchweek 10 the question has stayed the same but the answer has changed: Where is our league heading? According to Anadolu Agency and the TFF table, Galatasaray are top on 25 points; Amed Sportif Faaliyetler are second on 23; Trabzonspor third on 22 [1][2][3]. Anyone who looks at this table and reads only the scorelines misses the soul of the league.

This is my reading: the state of our league is no longer just a table; it is the story of which fans are holding their breath in which stand.

The summit has widened beyond the big three

The familiar big-three pack we have seen for ten weeks in the Süper Lig seems to have broken up. Beşiktaş are fourth on 19 points, Kasımpaşa fifth on 17, and Fenerbahçe, beaten 1-2 at home by Göztepe, have dropped to sixth on 16 [1][2]. By Habertürk's count, Galatasaray won all five of the games they played in 17 days between two international breaks: Gençlerbirliği, Lille, the Fenerbahçe derby, Konyaspor and Stuttgart [5]. So leadership is not a score; it is a rhythm of breathing.

On Saturday evening Galatasaray v Amed will be played at RAMS Park [4]. Perhaps it is not a classic derby; but it is a game that asks about the soul of the season. Because second-placed Amed stand ahead of Fenerbahçe and Beşiktaş in the table [1][3]. This time the stands will feel a different kind of tension from the usual scorelines: "Who will write the story of this league?"

This is my reading: the title race is still run at Galatasaray's tempo; but the league's heart no longer beats only in the noise of the Istanbul derby.

The weariness of European competition, on the league's shoulders

Midweek, European competition added to the load as well. In matchday four of the UEFA Europa League, as reported by Habertürk, Beşiktaş lost 3-2 at Celtic Park; their goals came from Junior Olaïtan and Orkun Kökçü [7]. In the same days Galatasaray had beaten Stuttgart 2-0 [5]. So on one side, a leader breathing freely in Europe; on the other, a Beşiktaş returning from Europe to host Gençlerbirliği at Tüpraş [4].

For a footballer, this tempo is not just distance covered. In the coach's office the team sheet, the injury list and the "who do I rest for this game" calculation sit side by side. And at the stadium gates the fans ask the same question: who is really ready tonight?

This is my reading: every team returning from Europe carries not only points into the league, but the patience of its supporters too.

The relegation zone and the transfer window: the price of silence

The bottom of the table is talked about less than the top, but it weighs more. According to Anadolu Agency's table, Samsunspor on 4 points and Eyüpspor on 3 occupy the last two places [1][2]. According to Habertürk, FIFA has imposed a three-window registration ban on Eyüpspor from 15 October 2026; while the ban lasts the club cannot register new players [6]. So as the January window approaches, a team in the relegation zone may find the option of strengthening its squad off the table.

That reads like a cold line from a rulebook. On the pitch it has another face: the weight on a young player's shoulders, the XI a coach builds from a thin squad, the brief hope in the stands after every goal. Every goal is born on the shoulders of an assist; but in the relegation zone that assist is sometimes only a breath away.

That is what you see when you look at our league's transfers and squads pages: not lists of names, but how each club will go into each window. The transfer window calendar is clear on paper; on the pitch, the only clear thing is the loneliness that clubs like Eyüpspor live with under a ban [6].

This is my reading: the end-of-season reckoning is not only done at the top. It is done in the relegation zone too; there the reckoning is quieter, and more human.

The derby script has been torn up, and the weekend brings a new test

According to Habertürk's summary, Galatasaray beat Fenerbahçe 2-1 in the derby on 26 October [5]. That night the classic eternal rivalry was rewritten. But the league's question did not end there. While our derby radar and derby calendar point to the traditional fixtures, the real tension of matchweek 11 is the first-versus-second meeting on Saturday at 19:00 [4]. On Sunday it is Fenerbahçe in Çorum, Trabzonspor in Alanya, and Beşiktaş v Gençlerbirliği at Tüpraş [4].

What you see on the Süper Lig form and fixtures pages is rhythm more than maths: who keeps winning, who will stumble after returning from Europe, who is looking for one more breath in the relegation zone.

This is my reading: the state of our league will not be summed up in a single sentence before the end of the season. What will be settled is whose stand gets condemned to silence.

Closing

After the tenth matchweek our league whispers this: the road to the title still runs through the top; but the soul of the league also runs through Amed's second place, the mark Göztepe left at Kadıköy and Eyüpspor's loneliness under a ban [1][5][6]. With the weariness of European competition on its shoulders, a high-tempo season continues between international windows [5].

This is my reading: when we ask where our league is heading, what we are really asking is this — is football in this country still only the derby of the big three, or is it rewritten in every game, every goal, every breath a fan takes?
