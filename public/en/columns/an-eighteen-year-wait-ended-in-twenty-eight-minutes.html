An Eighteen-Year Wait Ended in Twenty-Eight Minutes

FutbolRadar 27 August 2026

Fenerbahçe won 2-1 away at Lyon through goals from Vedat Muriqi and Archie Brown to reach the Champions League league phase after 18 years.

When the clock at Lyon's Groupama Stadium read 22:24 last night, the ball off Vedat Muriqi's foot hit the net. Four minutes later Archie Brown did the same thing. And in the middle of France, surrounded by tens of thousands of Lyon supporters, an eighteen-year wait was quietly doubled over: 2-0.

Writing these lines, I am still thinking about the four minutes between those two goals. In football the fate of a match sometimes fits not into ninety minutes but into four. Last night was one of those.

Courage, when it meets a plan

The 1-1 from the first leg in Istanbul had, for most pundits, left Fenerbahçe in trouble. There is no away-goals rule any more, true; but Lyon would be playing at home, in front of their own supporters, with nothing to lose. And so they played. They had more of the ball, knew how to move the game out of their own half, and with 57 per cent possession looked like the "owners" of the match.

Which is exactly why what the yellow-and-navies did is worth more. Fenerbahçe left Lyon the ball but did not leave them the game. They had eleven shots, all on target, against Lyon's nine. They took seven corners. In other words, they produced more threat with less of the ball. That is not a coincidence; that is a plan.

Look at the team: Ederson in goal, a back four of Semedo, Škriniar, Ake and Brown in front of him. Guendouzi, Kanté and Talisca in the middle. Greenwood, Muriqi and Oğuz Aydın ahead of them. That is not a team picked to hold on, it is a team picked to strike. The gaps Kanté closed in midfield, the timing of Talisca's releases to the man ahead of him, Brown getting far enough forward from left-back to score... Last night Fenerbahçe did not protect their own goal away from home, they went looking for the opponent's.

A story about a road

To remember how hard this tie was earned, you have to wind the calendar back a little. Górnik Zabrze in mid-July; 1-0 at Kadıköy, 1-1 in Poland. Sturm Graz at the start of August; 2-0 at home, 1-0 away in Austria. And now Lyon; 1-1 in Istanbul, 2-1 in France.

Six matches. Three rounds. Not a single defeat.

The line running from those first qualifiers played in the July heat, which nobody took very seriously, to today is really a description of this team's character. Fenerbahçe did not arrive at this round through an explosion but by laying one more brick every week.

The 61st minute

Let us be honest: Malick Fofana's goal on 61 minutes tied everybody's stomach in a knot, in the stands and in front of the screens. With the score at 2-1 it was obvious Lyon were preparing for a twenty-minute storm. The substitutions came one after another, the stadium rose, the tempo climbed.

And Fenerbahçe did not come apart.

That, really, is the most important sentence of this match. The thirty minutes after conceding were a harder examination than the two goals scored. Škriniar and Ake made no mistakes in the penalty area, Ederson was there at the critical moments, and Kanté worked like a first line of defence every time we lost the ball. Even the yellow cards in the closing minutes were a sign of deliberate game management rather than panic.

And those 437 tickets

The Lyon board cancelling the tickets of 437 Fenerbahçe supporters who had bought seats in the home end before the match was a strange story. A club being that wary of the noise of the opposition support... Frankly, it looks like a psychological defeat conceded before anyone took the field.

Those 437 people may not have been in the stands. But from Kadıköy to Ankara, from Berlin to Sydney, there were millions who rose to their feet at the same instant after those two goals. Tickets can be cancelled; belonging cannot.

What happens now?

Today's draw will decide Fenerbahçe's opponents in the league phase. The best in Europe will be coming to Kadıköy. Those who have forgotten for eighteen years what that anthem, that stadium and those nights mean will remember; and a generation that has never lived it will meet it for the first time.

The near-€40m in revenue heading into the accounts matters too, of course — it means this club can breathe, can do business, can spread its planning over the long term. But we all know that what happened in Lyon last night does not fit onto a balance sheet.

Fenerbahçe are back among the elite after eighteen years. And this time they did not get there by invitation, they fought their way in.

Welcome back, Kanarya. We have missed you badly.
