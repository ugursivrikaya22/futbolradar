Lille 2-4 Galatasaray: Lille Owned the Ball, Galatasaray Owned the Game

FutbolRadar 21 October 2026

Galatasaray, who broke at the start of the second half against Barcelona, broke the game open in exactly those minutes at Lille. But Lille's numbers also say this efficiency will not repeat itself every week.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

At the Pierre Mauroy, Lille had more of the ball, Lille took more shots, and Lille were ahead on expected goals too. The scoreboard, however, read Lille 2-4 Galatasaray [1][5]. Coming after defeats to Sporting Lisbon and Barcelona, this win gave the yellow-and-reds their first three points of the Champions League league phase [1][3]. Line-ups and match details are on our match page. This column looks at the turning points and the numbers behind that score.

How the game unfolded: four of the six goals in the first half

Okan Buruk made three changes from the starting XI that beat Gençlerbirliği 1-0 in the league: Sane, Lemina and Eren Elmalı dropped to the bench; Torreira, Yunus Akgün and Jakobs started [10]. Two of those changes touched the score directly.

Galatasaray got going in the second minute: Barış Alper Yılmaz's cross from the left deflected off a defender and Yunus Akgün sent it into the net: 0-1 [1][2]. At the press conference the day before, Yunus had said: "It was our coach Okan's birthday; we want to give him a win as a present" [7]. He delivered the first part of the present himself.

The lead lasted five minutes. In the 7th minute, from Alexsandro's pass in behind the defence, Soriba Diaoune went past Uğurcan Çakır and equalised [1][2]. The club's official match report notes that Roland Sallai lost his balance in that move [3].

27th-28th minute: the penalty overturned by VAR

At 1-1, Victor Osimhen got in behind the defence from Yunus Akgün's through ball and went down after a challenge from goalkeeper Berke Özer. Slovenian referee Rade Obrenovic first pointed to the spot; on VAR's advice he reviewed the incident, decided there was no foul and cancelled the penalty [1][2]. Habertürk recorded the moment in the 27th minute, AA in the 28th [1][2].

Whether the decision was right is a separate debate. What interested us more was the team's reaction: after that incident Galatasaray fell behind in the 39th minute. The ball rebounded off Uğurcan from Bakwa's shot, and from Haraldsson's pass Diaoune put it in: 2-1 [1][2]. The club's report writes that the attack came from a ball Gabriel Sara failed to close down because of a timing error [3].

The answer came before half-time. In the 44th minute, from Sallai's cross from the right, Aleksey Batrakov found the net with a header, and the first half ended 2-2 [1][2].

The Barcelona lesson: the first quarter of the second half

Before the game Buruk had asked for two things: "We want to spread the game over 90 minutes against Lille" and "We must play the start of second halves well, whatever the result" [7]. Behind those words was the Barcelona game a week earlier: that night, after a first half that finished 1-1, Galatasaray conceded twice in the 50th and 53rd minutes and lost 1-4 [9].

At Lille the same window worked the other way. In the 52nd minute, from Gabriel Sara's corner on the right, Lucas Torreira ran to the near post, and his header went in off Nabil Bentaleb: 2-3 [1][2]. Habertürk and AA recorded the goal as a Bentaleb own goal; the club's official website credits it to Torreira [1][2][3]. Whoever it is credited to, the goal came at almost exactly the same point as the two goals conceded against Barcelona.

Nine minutes later the lead was two. In a quick attack that began with Batrakov's long pass, Osimhen laid the ball off to Barış Alper Yılmaz, got it back from his pass and shot from just behind the penalty spot: 2-4 [2]. Barış Alper was thus involved in the build-up to both the first and the last goal [1][2]. According to the club's report, after that goal Lille created no significant chance apart from Kjaergaard's in the 82nd minute; a Galatasaray goal by Osimhen was ruled out for offside [3].

Osimhen: a goal at his old home, an apology and a standing ovation

According to UEFA's pre-match data, Osimhen scored 18 goals in 38 games for Lille in 2019/20 [6]. Tonight he rippled the net against his former club and, as Habertürk reported, apologised to the Lille fans after the goal. When he came off in the 84th minute, the Lille stands gave him a standing ovation [1].

Named player of the match according to the club's website, Osimhen said at full time: "I love this stadium very much. The fans are incredible. It was a tough game... I'm happy we took the three points" [4]. Osimhen was at the centre of three key moments in this game: the cancelled penalty, the goal he scored and the second goal ruled out for offside [1][2][3]. Galatasaray's ceiling in Europe still depends largely on his feet.

Batrakov: two games, two goals

Batrakov scored in a second straight Champions League game [1]; against Barcelona too he had scored the team's only goal in the 28th minute [8]. At Lille he added the long pass that led to the 61st-minute goal [2]. As we wrote a week ago (Barcelona scored four, Galatasaray were left with questions), a collection of stars is not a playing model. But in Galatasaray's last two European games, the most consistent figure in attack has not been one of the squad's most talked-about stars, but Batrakov.

The numbers: Lille played, Galatasaray finished

According to FotMob's match statistics, possession was Lille 56% – Galatasaray 44%, total shots 15-10, shots on target 5-4, expected goals (xG) 1.88-1.64 [5]. Lille were ahead on passes too: 524 to 408, with accuracy of 86% to 81% [5].

The numbers in Galatasaray's favour lie elsewhere: big chances 4 to 2, touches in the opposition box 27 to 22, and 66% (21 to 11) of aerial duels won by Galatasaray [5]. Two of the four goals, Batrakov's and Torreira's efforts, came from headers [1][2]. In other words, Galatasaray had little of the ball but took what they had into more dangerous areas.

The most striking pair is this: Galatasaray had 4 shots on target, with an expected goals on target (xGOT) value of 1.02; for Lille the figure was 2.13 [5]. Saves: Uğurcan Çakır 4, Berke Özer 1 [5]. These two lines say the same thing: Galatasaray finished far above the value of the chances that came their way (and one goal was an opponent's own goal), while Lille's shots on target were more dangerous and Uğurcan kept the game alive.

Runs that were broken

Galatasaray's winless run is over. According to UEFA, Galatasaray went into the game without a win in their last six Champions League league-phase games (1 draw, 5 defeats) and having lost their last five away games in the competition. They had also failed to win in their last six games in France (2 draws, 4 defeats) [6]. All three runs ended at Lille.

Lille's home pattern was broken. According to UEFA's data, Lille had scored first in their last eight home Champions League games and had led at half-time in their last six home games [6]. Tonight Galatasaray scored first in the 2nd minute, and the half ended 2-2 [1].

A bad record for Lille. The French side came into the game on four straight defeats in Europe; UEFA noted that the club had never lost five in a row [6]. This defeat was the fifth. Lille's six-game unbeaten run against Turkish opponents (2 wins, 4 draws) also came to an end [6].

Goals are never in short supply. According to UEFA, Galatasaray's last six Champions League games had produced 26 goals [6]; with the six at Lille, that number reached 32 in seven games. Goals are never missing in Galatasaray's games; the question is which net they go into.

According to the club's pre-match note, this was Galatasaray's first meeting with Lille in European competition, and they had 7 wins in 22 games against French sides [8]. With the win at Lille, that rose to 8 in 23.

Facing up to our preview

In our preview we noted that Lille had failed to keep a clean sheet in their last ten Champions League games and wrote: "Lille conceding looks almost certain; the question is whether Galatasaray can hold on in the game without conceding first." The question was answered in the second minute: Galatasaray scored first [1]. The number of games in which Lille have failed to keep a clean sheet has risen to eleven [6].

The table and the road ahead

Galatasaray have reached 3 points from three games; Lille are still without a point [1]. Before the game Buruk had said: "The 5 games we have left in the Champions League are very important... Every point is valuable for the next round" [7]; the first of those five has been won. The next Champions League game is at home to Stuttgart; before that there is the Fenerbahçe derby in the league [3]. Current table: Champions League standings. The derby opponent's European week: Fenerbahçe 2-2 Slavia Prague full-time analysis.

Final word: a way of playing found, or one good night?

This win is a partial answer to the question we asked after the Barcelona defeat: this time Galatasaray did not break at the start of the second half; they broke the game open. But both of Lille's goals came from balls played in behind the defence and from individual errors that the club's own report points to [2][3]. Lille took 15 shots, and the expected goals value of their shots on target was more than double Galatasaray's [5].

Giving away the same spaces against Stuttgart would mean relying on the same efficiency again. The real question is this: did Galatasaray find a way of playing at Lille, or did they just have a good night? The remaining Champions League games, starting with Stuttgart, will give the answer.
