Galatasaray's Game Plan

Galatasaray – Çorum FK · Build-up, width, the supply routes to Osimhen and how to open a compact block.

Possession and first-phase build-up

At Rams Park, on the opening weekend, against a side newly back in the Süper Lig, Galatasaray dominating the ball is the expected scenario. But possession alone is not an advantage: against a compact block it usually means patiently looking for space.

The critical detail in the first phase is where the defensive midfielders stand. If both sit on the same line, one opposition midfielder can cover them with a single cover shadow; if one drops and the other stays high, passing angles multiply and the direction of pressure becomes unclear. Gabriel Sara operating between the lines matters here: a player who can receive in the tight space between defence and midfield is the switch that turns a sideways passing chain forward.

Width, half-spaces and supplying Osimhen

There are two ways to open a compact block: width and depth. Width means switching quickly from one flank to the other and exploiting the defence's delay in shifting. Depth means pushing the block back with runs in behind.

Supplying Victor Osimhen sits where those two meet. The ball he is most effective with is an early flat cross into the box or a diagonal in behind. Looking for him to feet, back to goal, between three defenders is choosing the hardest option. Having players on the flanks who can cut inside and look for a shooting angle — Leroy Sané and Yunus Akgün among them — forces the defence into a choice: close the flank and the centre of the box opens; close the middle and the cross is free.

Barış Alper Yılmaz's ability to run in behind can serve a different function when the block drops: pinning the centre-backs deep to open space in front of Osimhen. What matters is not one name but the variety of runs around Osimhen — if they all arrive in the box at once, a single defensive block meets all of them together.

Full-backs and the counter-press

The full-backs' contribution is two-sided here. In attack they provide the width that frees the winger to come inside; defensively their high positions thin out the rest defence. A physically strong full-back with real recovery pace — Wilfried Singo among them — can carry that balance alone, but relying on one player's recovery is not a systemic solution.

That makes the pressure applied the instant the ball is lost part of the plan rather than an extra. The aim is not only to win it straight back; forcing the opponent's first pass sideways or backwards is enough. A midfielder who covers ground, such as Lesley Ugochukwu, closes the vertical option in that first second and buys the defence time to reset.
