Surrender at Papara Park: Okan Buruk's Ninety Minutes Without an Answer

FutbolRadar 20 September 2026

Süper Lig matchday 6 | Trabzonspor 4-0 Galatasaray | Papara Park, 19 September 2026

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Some defeats you shrug off as the way football goes. Others hang on the wall like a photograph. The picture taken at Papara Park on the evening of 19 September does not tell you what Galatasaray are this season — it tells you who is managing them.

The fourth minute. Before the noise in the stands had settled, before the notebooks on the touchline were open, Mohamed Salah turned in Mustafa Eskihellaç's pass. After that goal, the only thing Galatasaray did on the pitch was try to understand what was happening to them. Saviolo on 39, Salah again on 44. They went in at half-time three down.

"We had the ball" is no longer a defence

The statistical record of this match dismantles every line of defence built around this team in recent weeks. Galatasaray had 54% of the ball. They took 13 shots. That sounds like the data of a side applying pressure, doesn't it?

Now look at the other column. Only 3 of those 13 shots hit the target — an accuracy of 23%. Expected goals: 0.86. Expected goals per shot: 0.066. Every attempt Galatasaray produced was worth far less than an average chance.

Trabzonspor took 7 shots, 4 on target, and generated 2.28 xG — 0.326 per shot. When the opposition is creating chances five times better than yours, possession is not superiority. It is an excuse. And that excuse has been used for a long time now.

This squad contains Sané, Sara, Torreira, Batrakov. If those names produce three shots on target in ninety minutes, the problem is not the quality of the players. The problem is the idea meant to bind that quality together. At Papara Park there was no idea.

The real issue was the triple change on 68 minutes

The cruellest test of management is the response when things go wrong. Okan Buruk answered that test by looking at the clock, not the pitch.

Three goals down at the interval, he made a single substitution. A team that had conceded three and threatened nothing carried on with one change. Then, on 68 minutes, with the scoreline long settled, three players came off at once. The fifth change arrived on 73.

That is not a plan. That is a signature on a match already spent. Had those three changes come twenty minutes earlier, while the game still had a pulse, we could at least say he tried. A change made on 68 is not a move; it is an acceptance. The supporters were still shouting in the stands. The bench had already surrendered.

The rest we all saw. Salah completed his hat-trick on 80. Ugochukwu was sent off on 87 after VAR upgraded his card. Sané was booked for a foul in stoppage time. These are not the closing minutes of a football match; they are the closing minutes of a team that has lost its discipline, its concentration and its reason. When a side loses heart, the cards come first. The man who prepares that side sits on the bench.

Not a bad day — an alarm that has been ringing for weeks

This result was no surprise, because the warnings were there. Galatasaray beat Göztepe 3-2. They won 3-2 away at Başakşehir. They lost 3-1 to Sporting. This team has been conceding for weeks, playing uncontrolled matches for weeks — but individual quality in attack kept rescuing the result, so the problem stayed invisible.

At Papara Park the individual quality fell silent, and what remained was the tactical skeleton. It turned out there wasn't one. A manager whose side concedes four has to answer this: over ninety minutes, what solution was found for Salah drifting free in the box three separate times? None. All four Trabzonspor goals were assisted — these were not flashes of individual brilliance but the same attacking pattern, built again and again.

Okan Buruk has won titles at this club, and nobody is trying to erase that. But football's memory feeds on ninety minutes, not on trophies. And in these ninety minutes, Galatasaray supporters saw nothing resembling anything won in the past.

Final word

Four goals, one red card, three shots on target. You cannot explain that with an injury, a refereeing decision or bad luck. You can only explain it with preparation — or the lack of it.

Galatasaray's problem tonight was not losing. The problem was losing without trying anything. A team is allowed to lose; it is not allowed to surrender. And the man who teaches a team not to surrender is the one in the dugout.

The question Okan Buruk has to answer is this: does this team have an idea, or does it merely have good footballers? At Papara Park we all saw the answer. The full record of the night is in the match page, and the current table is on the Süper Lig standings page.
