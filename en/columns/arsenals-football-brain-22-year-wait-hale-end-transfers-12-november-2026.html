Arsenal's Football Brain: How a 22-Year Wait Is Turning Into a Dynasty

FutbolRadar 12 November 2026

Premier League champions Arsenal are top again with 27 points from 10 games. Arteta's discipline, Berta's transfer table and the Hale End academy tell one story: a football brain.

You judge a team's football brain not by the trophy it wins but by how it gets up after the final it loses.

FutbolRadar

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Arsenal are no longer the "almost" team. Last season they ended a 22-year wait for the league title [1] and came home from the Champions League final beaten only on penalties [2]; ten games into this season they are top again with 27 points [14]. Explaining that with money alone would be lazy.

What has been built in north London over the years is a football brain: the patience to wait for the right man, a discipline that does not make mistakes, a cool head at the transfer table when buying and when selling, and an academy money cannot buy.

22 years, three second places and a night in Budapest

When Wenger's "Invincibles" won the league without losing a game in 2004, nobody imagined the next title would take 22 years. Then came the bill for the move from Highbury to the Emirates, a club selling its stars, Wenger's long final years and FA Cups as consolation. The 2006 Champions League final, lost to Barcelona in Paris, stayed the club's only big European memory for a long time.

When Mikel Arteta arrived in December 2019 he inherited a team that had lost its way. From 2022-23 they finished second three seasons in a row, and the English press never let go of the "bottle it on the run-in" joke.

2025-26 buried it. Arsenal won the league with 85 points, seven clear of Manchester City. The irony is that the title arrived without them kicking a ball: on 19 May, when City drew 1-1 at Bournemouth [5]. The trophy was lifted on the final day after a 2-1 win over Crystal Palace at Selhurst Park.

Then Budapest. In the Champions League final on 30 May, Havertz scored in the opening minutes, Dembélé equalised from the spot in the second half and it finished 1-1 after 120 minutes. Eze and Gabriel missed in the shootout and PSG won 4-3 [2]. Arsenal had very little of the ball that night, yet for two hours they forced the European champions to play their game.

With the League Cup final lost too, the season went into the record books as one trophy and two scars [3]. One wait is over, another has taken its place: Europe.

The football brain: a team that does not make mistakes

Arteta's talk of "the process" was mocked for years. Now the numbers do the talking, and the story they tell is one of discipline.

No red cards, no penalties. Arsenal became the first team in Premier League history to finish a season without a red card and without conceding a penalty. The season before, they had six players sent off and gave away three penalties.

A team that does not lose when ahead. They won 24 of the 27 league games in which they scored first and lost only one. Twenty-five league games were decided by a single goal; the most common score was a 1-0 win, eight times [4].

Set pieces as a weapon. They broke the Premier League record for goals from corners. They conceded the fewest goals in the league; the Saliba–Gabriel partnership and Raya, Golden Glove winner for a third season in a row, were the foundations of the title [3][5].

This is not a manifesto for pretty football; it is the knowledge of how to win. Arteta has reached 25 or more league wins in three of the last four seasons, as often as Wenger did at Arsenal. That is what a football brain means: turning a game into the kind of game you want and taking mistakes down to almost zero.

The transfer table: an Arsenal that learned to buy and to sell

The new era began in the office. In March 2025 Andrea Berta, after 12 years at Atlético Madrid, became sporting director [8]. The Italian, who had a hand in signing Griezmann, Rodri and Oblak, said from day one that he had come to work with Arteta and win immediately.

Summer 2025 was a statement: Gyökeres, Zubimendi, Eze, Madueke and Mosquera arrived, and Hincapié came on loan. Saying every deal worked would be a lie. Gyökeres was top scorer with 21 goals but faded in the big games [4]; the signing of the season was the quiet arrival of Mosquera [3]. Still, Zubimendi's 57-game season showed the centre of midfield had been built right.

The real change came in summer 2026. For years Arsenal were known as the club that could not sell; this summer they raised roughly £118 million from seven players, going by reported fees [11][12][15]:

The one missing piece is a big centre-forward; the deal linked with names like Álvarez and Vinícius but never done is January's top file.

Hale End: the thing money cannot buy

Arsenal's most valuable asset is not a signing but the Hale End academy in north London. Bukayo Saka came through it and became a world star. In the 2024-25 injury crisis Myles Lewis-Skelly and Ethan Nwaneri stepped up and became the answer [7].

Then came Max Dowman. He made his Premier League debut against Leeds at 15 years and 235 days; the only younger player in Premier League history is another Hale End boy, Nwaneri [7]. Last season he became the youngest scorer in Premier League history at 16 years and 73 days, and by the end of it the youngest player ever to win the league [6].

This is where Arteta is different: he met players like Nwaneri and Lewis-Skelly in person years before they reached the first team. The club tied both to long contracts [7].

But in a title-winning squad the path narrows. Nwaneri struggled on loan at Marseille last season and this season has been loaned to Borussia Dortmund [15]. Note the irony: on 24 November Dortmund visit the Emirates in the Champions League. Unless his loan contract says otherwise, Nwaneri could come home in the opposition's shirt.

The view from Turkey: Trossard and a lesson for the Süper Lig

Arsenal's new thinking has reached our league as well. In July Leandro Trossard joined Beşiktaş; Arsenal received £17 million and the player signed a three-year deal with an option for a fourth [12][15]. Selling a 31-year-old entering the final year of his contract, with a winner's medal and at a good price, instead of losing him for nothing: that is the new Arsenal line.

Trossard has not stood still in Istanbul either. He scored his first goal in black and white against Erzurumspor FK in week five and found the net again away at Kasımpaşa on 1 November. Tonight in İzmir he is one of Belgium's weapons against Türkiye.

The lesson for the Süper Lig is simple but painful. Arsenal waited 22 years, yet in the last seven of them they did not change their manager. They promoted academy players to the first team, sold a 31-year-old star in his final year and even let 25-year-old Martinelli go when the right price arrived [11]. Here, coaches and squads are rebuilt most summers, and stars past 30 arrive on the highest wages.

This season: top spot, one shock and a 12-day test

In the first ten games Arsenal won nine and collected 27 points; their only defeat was a 3-0 loss at Brighton on 19 September. They won 1-0 at Anfield on 1 November, and City's defeat at Forest opened the gap [14]:

Not everything has been smooth. On 27 October they went out of the League Cup on penalties to fourth-tier Fleetwood Town; Max Dowman was sent off in that game and the club appealed [13]. Saliba is also out for months with a back injury, which is why Konsa was bought for £51 million [10].

Final word

Calling Arsenal "almost" is now unfair. What they learned over 22 years fits into four words: patience, discipline, smart trading and trust in your own.

The gaps are clear too: a European trophy and a striker who decides big games. But you judge a team's football brain not by the trophy it wins but by how it gets up after the final it loses. Five months after Budapest, Arsenal are top again.
