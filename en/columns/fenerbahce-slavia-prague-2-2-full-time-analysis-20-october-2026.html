Fenerbahçe 2-2 Slavia Prague: A Lead Built in 11 Minutes Was Gone in the 90th, and the Lock Stayed Shut

FutbolRadar 20 October 2026

Leading at half-time for the first time in 13 games, Fenerbahçe let three points slip at the death through midfield errors and missed chances.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

One story was written at Kadıköy for 86 minutes, and another in the final minutes. In matchday 3 of the Champions League league phase, Fenerbahçe were 2-0 up on Slavia Prague by the 11th minute. But the game ended 2-2; the equaliser came in the 90th minute [1][2]. Details are on our match page. This column looks at the numbers and mistakes behind that score.

How the game unfolded: 2-0 in 11 minutes, 2-1 four minutes later

Fenerbahçe could not have started better. In the 4th minute Oğuz Aydın cut inside from the right and shot from distance, and the ball flew into the top corner: 1-0. In the 11th minute Mason Greenwood got into the box from Semedo's pass, steadied himself and hit it hard: 2-0 [2].

But Fenerbahçe enjoyed that lead for only four minutes. In the 15th minute Matteo Guendouzi, receiving Ederson's pass on the edge of the box, misplaced a pass; Provod intercepted it and pulled one back one-on-one [2]. Six minutes later came more bad news: Nathan Ake was injured and made way for Kojo Oppong [1][3].

For the rest of the first half Fenerbahçe looked for a third: Guendouzi's shot in the 27th minute went narrowly over, Oğuz Aydın hit the side netting in the 37th, and Markovic saved Vedat Muriqi's effort in the 45th [2]. The half ended 2-1 [1].

Second half: two clear chances missed, and the final minute

In the second half the balance of the game shifted. According to NTV's commentary, Guendouzi lost the ball in his own half again in the 54th minute and Provod's shot grazed the post. In the 61st minute Oppong cleared Provod's effort before it crossed the line [3].

In the 63rd minute Kartal took off Muriqi and Oğuz and sent on Romelu Lukaku and Nene [1]. The change produced chances but not goals: in the 64th minute, from Lukaku's pass, Nene could not beat the goalkeeper one-on-one; in the 82nd minute, from Greenwood's pass, Lukaku chose to shoot and missed the target [1][3]. Ederson stopped Ayaosi in the 81st minute and Zima in the 88th [3]. And in the 90th minute, from Ayaosi's cross, Kačor put the ball in from six yards: 2-2 [1][3].

The disciplinary record was harsh too: for Fenerbahçe, Kanté (31'), Škriniar (49') and İsmail Yüksek (88') were booked; for Slavia, Nowak (60') and two members of the coaching staff were shown cards [1].

The numbers: what was broken, what goes on

The winless run goes on. According to UEFA, Fenerbahçe went into the game on a nine-game winless run in the Champions League (3 draws, 6 defeats) [4]. With this draw the run stretched to 10 games (4 draws, 6 defeats). Their last win is still the 2008 game against Chelsea.

One run was broken. UEFA had written that Fenerbahçe had not led at half-time in any of their last 12 Champions League games [4]. Tonight the half ended 2-1 to Fenerbahçe [1]. So this time the problem was not the start, but the finish.

The rule of not losing at home after scoring first still stands. Fenerbahçe had never lost a home Champions League game in which they scored first (7 wins, 1 draw) [4]. That rule was not broken, but a second draw came.

Slavia's runs. The Czech side had gone 21 games without a win in the Champions League and had never won a game in which they conceded first (1 draw, 12 defeats) [4]. Both runs continue; but Slavia took their first point of this season's league phase.

The table. Fenerbahçe had collected 1 point in the first two matchdays [5]. This result takes them to 2 points; Slavia move off zero with 1 point. Current table: Champions League standings.

Tactical reading: Greenwood worked as a number 10, the midfield did not

Kartal kept his pre-match word: he gave Greenwood "a different responsibility", and according to NTV the player played as a number 10, in a free role, for the first time [3]. The result was a goal and the pass to Lukaku in the 82nd minute [1][2]. That role is clearly productive.

The real problem was in central midfield. Slavia's man-to-man pressing paid off exactly where we feared it would: on the first passes out from the back. Guendouzi's two losses of possession in the 15th and 54th minutes produced Slavia's two most dangerous chances [2][3]. Ake's early exit changed the balance of the defence. Oppong's intervention in the 61st minute saved the score, but in the 90th minute nobody could cut out the cross to the back post [3].

Facing up to our prediction

In our preview (readable here) we wrote that the most likely scenario was "a home win in which Fenerbahçe score first". We were right about the first goal and wrong about the result. Our second scenario was a draw; but we had described it as a game tied up by Slavia's pressing. Instead, the scoring opened early and the game was levelled in the final minute. Our "patience" warning also turned out the other way round: Fenerbahçe led at half-time, and what they lost was not patience but concentration. Our reading of Greenwood's new role and of Kanté being on the pitch did turn out to be correct [1][3].

The European maths and Monday

Before the game Kartal had said: "If we take 3 points, we will be more hopeful about the top 24." Instead of 3 points came 1, and Fenerbahçe have 2 points from three games. That means the margin for error in the remaining five games has narrowed considerably. Ake's injury has also raised a new question mark in defence ahead of the Galatasaray derby six days later [1].

Final word

Tonight Fenerbahçe came very close to opening the 18-year lock in the Champions League: they were 2-0 up, led at half-time and, thanks to goalkeeping saves, made it to the final minute. But at this level you have to manage a game not for 90 minutes but for 95. The lock is still shut; the key, though, is now clear: don't lose the ball in midfield.
