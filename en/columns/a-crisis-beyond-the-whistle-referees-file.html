A Crisis Beyond the Whistle: How Will Turkish Football Trust Its Referees Again?

FutbolRadar 02 October 2026

Opinion / Agenda | The "Referees File" investigation: the MHK, the VAR room and institutional trust

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Update · 03 November 2026 at 12:00

Asked about the resignations at the federation after the arrests of Gündoğdu and Yasin Kol, TFF President İbrahim Ethem Hacıosmanoğlu said, as reported by Sabah, that he was "not tired" and would remain in his post. Hacıosmanoğlu said that stepping down while there is suspicion could mean being part of the affair. No development regarding an indictment, release or new detentions in the investigation was reported.

Update · 27 October 2026 at 13:00

According to CNN Türk reporter Ümit Uzun, a hard disk found in the office used by branch chairman Ferdi Karadoruk and Provincial Referee Committee chairman Emre Kosif at the TFFHGD Istanbul Branch contained referee lists as well as score and ranking lists; red notes were reportedly added next to some names. The findings were reported to have emerged in the police examination. This information has not yet been confirmed by an official statement or an indictment, and the content of the notes has not been made public.

Update · 21 October 2026 at 21:00

The first indictment in the investigation has been prepared. According to NTV, the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office Illegal Betting and Sports Crimes Bureau drew up an indictment against detained TFFHGD Istanbul Branch chairman Ferdi Karadoruk, seeking a prison sentence of 1 year 3 months to 8 years on charges including "violation of the Associations Law". Whether the indictment has been accepted by the court has not yet been reported; the presumption of innocence applies until the trial is concluded.

Update · 20 October 2026 at 17:00

The TFF reviewed the vacant seats on the Central Referee Committee and officially announced that Anthony Taylor, who had until then served as Elite Referee Director, had been appointed MHK chairman. Alan Snoddy, Igor Radojicic and Barış Şimşek were also appointed committee members. This resolves the lack of an official statement on Taylor's role that we noted on 12 October; no statement on the length of the appointment was reported.

Update · 20 October 2026 at 16:00

According to a report by NOW Spor reporter Ersin Düzen, published by Sporx, seven referees filed a criminal complaint with the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office against TFF President İbrahim Ethem Hacıosmanoğlu and former MHK chairman Ferhat Gündoğdu, who resigned from his post. The petition reportedly includes various allegations, chiefly defamation and abuse of office. These are the complainants' allegations; how the prosecutor's office will assess the complaint is not yet known, and no statement from the TFF on the matter was reported.

Update · 13 October 2026 at 16:00

According to Sözcü on 13 October, UEFA referee observer Dilan Deniz Gökçek İşcan filed a criminal complaint against former MHK chairman Ferhat Gündoğdu and two other people. İşcan alleges that an untrue excuse letter was sent to UEFA in her name regarding an international assignment in 2022. These are the complainant's allegations; the response of those named in the complaint has not yet been reported in the press, and it is not known whether the allegations will be merged with the existing "Referees File" investigation.

Update · 12 October 2026 at 16:00

According to Habertürk on 12 October, citing HT Spor editor-in-chief Ahmet Selim Kul, MHK Elite Referee Director Anthony Taylor will continue in his role at the MHK until the end of the season; former referee Alan Snoddy will also work alongside Taylor on refereeing matters. At the time this note was written, the TFF had not yet made an official statement on this, and Taylor's official title is given as "MHK Elite Referee Director".

Update · 09 October 2026 at 20:00

According to NTV Spor on 9 October, after an extraordinary meeting in Riva the TFF Board decided not to hold an extraordinary election and to remain in office until the date of the ordinary elective general assembly. Following the decision, Vice-President Mecnun Otyakmaz and board members Mustafa Bozbağ, Zafer Bahadır Saraç, Bilal Arslan and Murat Şahin resigned. According to the TFF's statement, substitute members İbrahim Özbay and Metin Akyüz were appointed to two previously vacated full seats, and Ahmet Erzurumlu was appointed general secretary. At the time this note was written, no official statement had been made on a new MHK chairman.

Update · 09 October 2026 at 08:00

According to Hürriyet on 8 October, the referee appointments for Süper Lig matchweek 7 were announced. After Ferhat Gündoğdu's resignation as MHK chairman, the TFF had announced that the Süper Lig appointment process would be conducted in line with the technical assessments of MHK Elite Referee Director Anthony Taylor. At the time this note was written, the TFF had not yet announced an official appointment of a permanent MHK chairman.

Update · 08 October 2026 at 16:00

According to Habertürk on 8 October, citing journalist Tahir Kum on HT Spor, MHK VAR Coordinator Yaşar Kemal Uğurlu handed in his resignation letter and the federation processed it. The TFF made no official statement on the matter, and the reason for the resignation was not disclosed. Habertürk also reported that the TFF Board's extraordinary meeting would be held in Riva on Friday 9 October at 14:00.

Update · 08 October 2026 at 08:00

According to Anadolu Agency on 7 October, the TFF announced that General Secretary Abdullah Ayaz had resigned from his post on 5 October. The federation's statement gave no reason for the resignation, and a new general secretary has not yet been named. The resignation was among the changes of personnel in the federation's management while the "Referees File" investigation continues; the TFF's statement did not link the resignation to the investigation.

Update · 07 October 2026 at 20:00

According to Habertürk on 6 October, following developments in the "Referees File" investigation, the TFF Board will meet on Friday 9 October with an extraordinary election on the agenda. At the time this note was written, the TFF had not announced an official appointment of a new MHK chairman and members; referee appointments for Ziraat Turkish Cup games are being made by an interim panel.

Update · 07 October 2026 at 08:00

According to NTV on 6 October, the Turkish Association of Active Football Referees and Observers announced that Ferdi Karadoruk, Alican Sağlar and Emre Kosif, who were arrested as part of the investigation, had been expelled from the association. The association said the decision was taken following "an assessment of the process's impact on institutional reputation and standing". The expulsion is an internal administrative decision of the association; it is not a judicial ruling on the outcome of the criminal investigation.

Update · 06 October 2026 at 20:00

In a statement on NTV on 6 October, Justice Minister Akın Gürlek said the investigation covered allegations of forgery of official documents, mobbing and favouritism in appointments, adding: "Turkish football must be clean"; according to Fanatik, the minister also said of the suspects that "we saw they were not fair". A ministerial statement is not a judicial ruling; the investigation is continuing and the presumption of innocence applies to all suspects.

Update · 05 October 2026 at 20:00

According to Milliyet, the duty criminal judgeship of peace ordered the arrest of referee Yasin Kol on charges of "match-fixing" and "incentive", and of assistant referee Göktuğ Hazar Erel on a charge of "forgery of official documents". Kol said he denied the charges and believed the MASAK findings in the file were wrong. Arrest is a protective measure and does not mean a conviction; the presumption of innocence applies to both.

Update · 05 October 2026 at 16:00

According to Habertürk, after his processing at the prosecutor's office on 5 October, referee Yasin Kol was referred to the duty criminal judgeship of peace with a request for arrest. Yeni Şafak reported that the request was made on charges of "match-fixing and incentive bonuses" for Kol and "forgery of official documents" for assistant referee Göktuğ Hazar Erel. Kol had said in his police statement that he did not bet. A request for arrest is not a judicial ruling; at the time this note was written, the judgeship's decision had not yet been announced.

Update · 04 October 2026 at 20:00

According to NTV on 3 October, the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office filed a lawsuit seeking the dissolution of the Istanbul Branch of the Turkish Association of Active Football Referees and Observers under Article 89 of the Turkish Civil Code. The petition reportedly includes the allegation that referees were classified as "close, obedient, controllable and others" and that membership and loyalty, rather than objective criteria, were the basis for appointments. A request for dissolution means a case has been opened; the decision rests with the court. Yasin Kol and Göktuğ Hazar Erel are expected to be referred to the courthouse on 5 October.

Update · 04 October 2026 at 08:00

According to Milliyet, the arrested Ferhat Gündoğdu resigned as MHK chairman through his lawyers as of 3 October; the TFF reportedly received the written resignation. Sebahattin Şahin, released under judicial control, was also reported to have resigned from the MHK. According to the lawyers' statement, the judgeship rejected the request for arrest on the charges of "abuse of office" and "threats", and ordered the arrest on the charge of "recording personal data". At the time this note was written, no judicial decision had been announced regarding Yasin Kol and Göktuğ Hazar Erel.

Update · 03 October 2026 at 16:00

In a statement on its official website, the TFF announced that until a new Central Referee Committee is formed, referee appointments for the Ziraat Turkish Cup games on 6-8 October would be made by MHK Vice-Chairman Hikmet Öksüzoğlu and committee members Ali Zağlı, Kamil Abitoğlu, İbrahim Çınar and Ziya Çetin. The federation said work on forming the new MHK was continuing. At the time this note was written, no official appointment had been announced regarding the names mentioned in the press for the new chairmanship.

Update · 03 October 2026 at 12:00

According to Hürriyet, of the seven suspects referred with a request for arrest, the duty criminal judgeship of peace ordered the arrest of MHK chairman Ferhat Gündoğdu, Ferdi Karadoruk, Yunus Yıldırım, Alican Sağlar and Emre Kosif, and the release of Ahmet Şahin and Sebahattin Şahin under judicial control. Arrest is a protective measure and does not mean a conviction; the presumption of innocence applies to all suspects. At the time this note was written, no judicial decision had been announced regarding referee Yasin Kol and assistant referee Göktuğ Hazar Erel, who were detained separately.

Update · 03 October 2026 at 08:00

According to Habertürk, on the night of 3 October the prosecutor's office referred seven suspects, including MHK chairman Ferhat Gündoğdu, to the duty criminal judgeship of peace with a request for arrest. The request reportedly includes charges, varying by suspect, of "abuse of office", "threats", "forgery of official documents", "breach of trust in the course of duty" and "unlawful recording of personal data". A request for arrest is not a judicial ruling; at the time this note was written, the judgeship's decision had not yet been announced.

Update · 02 October 2026 at 16:00

Seven suspects, including MHK chairman Ferhat Gündoğdu, who were detained on 29 September, were referred to the Istanbul Courthouse on the morning of 2 October. Gündoğdu reportedly denied the charges in his statement. At the time this column was published, no judicial decision had been announced regarding the suspects; this section will be updated as developments occur.

The refereeing debate in Turkish football is not new. An incident every weekend, a press conference every Monday, and at the end of every season the sentence "the referees decided the season". We have become so used to this debate that we started to treat distrust of refereeing as the natural noise of football.

This time it is different. What is being discussed now is not an incident frozen on a screen. What is being discussed is an investigation file run by the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office. The debate has moved from the television studio to the courthouse corridor.

This column will not say who is guilty. That is for the court to say. But there is a question that needs to be asked: is this file about a few people, or is it an X-ray of a system that has operated behind closed doors for years?

What happened in a week?

According to Anadolu Agency, a simultaneous operation was carried out in Istanbul, Ankara, Elazığ and Manisa on 29 September. Seven people were detained, including Central Referee Committee (MHK) chairman Ferhat Gündoğdu.

Among those detained were TFF Active Referees' Association chairman Ferdi Karadoruk, former MHK vice-chairman Ahmet Şahin, former MHK member Yunus Yıldırım and other figures working within the refereeing organisation.

The investigation is being run by the Chief Prosecutor's Illegal Betting and Sports Crimes Investigation Bureau. The file was reported to include charges of "torment" and "forgery of official documents". At the centre of the allegations are complaints that referee rankings, appointments, scoring and promotion processes, as well as the laws-of-the-game exams, were interfered with.

On the same day, teams from the Istanbul Police Financial Crimes Unit carried out a search of the TFF's VAR room. The next day the TFF issued a statement confirming the search and said the process had been conducted with the knowledge and participation of officials in the federation's relevant units.

In the same statement, however, the TFF also flatly denied a claim circulating on social media: it said that reports that digital material in the units dealing with refereeing had been deleted before the search and recovered by police technical teams were "completely unfounded". The federation also announced that it reserved its legal rights against such publications. This denial deserves to be heard at least as loudly as the claim.

On 1 October the file widened. After examination of digital material obtained from the previously detained suspects and of MASAK records, Süper Lig referee Yasin Kol and assistant referee Göktuğ Hazar Erel were detained.

According to Hürriyet, the charges against the two are "violation of Law No. 7258", "participation in the crime of document forgery" and "abuse of office". Law No. 7258 is the law regulating betting and games of chance in sports competitions. In his statement as reported in the press, Yasin Kol reportedly said he did not bet and that he had sent money to some people as loans. At this point we have an allegation and a defence; there is no judicial ruling.

On 2 October seven suspects, including Gündoğdu, were referred to the Istanbul Courthouse. Gündoğdu reportedly denied the charges against him in his statement.

Referees are now in the witness queue

The detail that takes this file beyond an ordinary "operation story" is this: the people feeding the investigation are largely the referees themselves.

According to Anadolu Agency, before and after the operation many serving and former referees gave statements as complainants and witnesses at the Financial Crimes Unit. Among them are former referees who officiated at the highest level for many years, such as Deniz Ateş Bitnel, Hüseyin Göçek, Mete Kalkavan, Süleyman Abay and Alper Ulusoy.

The meaning is clear. People from inside the system have taken to the prosecutor's office a complaint that could not be resolved through the system's own channels. If an institution's internal oversight mechanism worked, would its own members be knocking on the door of the police?

The message records that have reached the public make this question bigger. AA reported that the prosecutor's file contains correspondence including requests for exam questions to be passed to a person. NTV reported that the file includes WhatsApp messages between the MHK chairman and a Süper Lig referee in which referee and VAR appointments were discussed.

The court will decide what these messages do and do not prove. But in a system where the public does not know by what criteria appointments are made, the mere appearance of such a message is enough on its own to shake trust.

The real question: a few people, or a whole system?

The easy path is obvious: a few names are removed from their posts, a new MHK is formed, and it is declared that "the page has been turned". Turkish football has watched this film before. Committees changed, chairmen changed; the distrust did not.

Because the problem lies not only in individuals but in the unlimited discretion granted to individuals. Does anyone today know by what criterion a referee moves up a ranking, with what score he drops down, or why a particular name is appointed to a derby? Where are the observer reports? How often, and under whose supervision, are VAR recordings assessed?

None of these questions has a public answer. Every question without an answer turns into a new conspiracy theory every weekend. Where there is no transparency, gossip becomes institutionalised.

The story of VAR is the most striking example of this contradiction. The technology came to end the arguments and make decisions visible. Today, however, the VAR room is in the headlines as the place where a financial crimes unit carried out a search. Technology alone does not produce trust; as long as it is not visible who uses that technology, under which rules and under what oversight, the screens remain part of the closed doors too.

In fact, it is transparency that protects the referee. A referee who rises according to the rules and is assessed by recorded criteria finds a system behind him when he is put in the crosshairs of club presidents. Today, the well-intentioned referee and the ill-intentioned allegation disappear into the same darkness.

This debate is not any club's cause. Galatasaray, Fenerbahçe, Beşiktaş, Trabzonspor or the Anatolian clubs: all their fans deserve to be able to look at the same whistle with the same trust. Turning this file into ammunition for club rivalries would deepen the problem rather than solve it.

What should be done?

Waiting for the outcome of the investigation is what the law requires. But we don't have to wait for that outcome to fix the system. The steps that can be taken are clear:

1. Exam and promotion criteria must be made public. The question security of the laws-of-the-game exams, who prepares them and how they are marked must be bound to an independent protocol. Ranking and promotion criteria must be published at the start of the season.

2. Appointment criteria must be shared with the public. Which ranking of referee is appointed to which game, and on what grounds, must be defined by a regulation. The committee's discretion should only be exercised within written criteria.

3. Referee and VAR performance data must be published regularly. At set intervals during the season, correct and incorrect decision rates, VAR intervention statistics and summaries of observer notes must be published.

4. An independent oversight mechanism must be established. A board of lawyers and former referees, independent of the federation's management and the clubs, should regularly review the MHK's decisions; a safe reporting channel should be opened for complaints.

5. Oversight of betting activity must be continuous and technology-based. The betting connections of referees and the refereeing organisation must be monitored continuously, not just when a scandal breaks. Data analysis and cooperation with the relevant institutions must become routine.

6. MHK decision-making must be put on record. Appointment, ranking and disciplinary decisions must be taken with reasoned minutes, and those minutes must be open to review. Informal correspondence between a committee chairman and a referee must never take the place of a decision-making process.

7. The training and assessment system must be restructured. Observation, scoring and training should be spread across separate units that check one another, rather than held in a single pair of hands. Developing referees must be based on measurable performance, not personal loyalty.

Final word

At the end of the investigation some names may be cleared, and some may be tried. The law will do its work; and the presumption of innocence applies to everyone.

But one truth stands regardless of the court's ruling: Turkish football's trust in its referees will not come back by removing a few people from their posts. Trust comes back when we can see how decisions are made.

The whistle blows on the pitch. But whether we trust that whistle is determined off the pitch, by decisions taken in closed rooms. It is time to open the doors of those rooms.
