A Success Story at Acun Ilıcalı's Hull City: A Start That Has Them Close to the Summit

FutbolRadar 07 September 2026

Hull City beat Manchester United 2-0 at home in the opening weeks of the Premier League season, drew 0-0 with Aston Villa and have still not conceded a goal. For the club Acun Ilıcalı took over in 2022, this is the first real dividend of a project built patiently.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Anyone glancing at the Premier League table after three matchdays could be forgiven for assuming it contains a typo: sitting third, immediately behind Manchester City and Arsenal and ahead of dozens of far wealthier clubs, are Hull City. Three games, two wins, one draw, no defeats — and not a single goal conceded along the way.

It would be easy to file all this under "early-season anomaly" and move on. But if you want to look for coincidence in Hull City's story, you have to start with a change-of-ownership agreement signed three and a half years ago. In January 2022 one of English football's old, long-overlooked institutions passed into the hands of a Turkish entrepreneur: Acun Ilıcalı.

Giving a Club Back to Itself

Ilıcalı's first significant act at Hull City was not a signing. He closed the long-running dispute over the crest, the one the previous board had tried to change years earlier and the supporters had never accepted; the club's historic badge and identity came back. The gesture looked small, but the message was not: the new ownership would not sever the club from its supporters — it would reattach them.

The decisions that followed — a rebuilt academy site, a bigger transfer budget, a coaching structure shaped patiently but decisively — were all parts of a long-term plan. In English football most projects of this kind either run out of money early or lose their bearings under the pressure for instant results. Hull City's is still standing three and a half years on, and it is now paying out on the pitch.

Behind the Pre-Season: A Camp That Ran Through Türkiye

The route the club took into this season said plenty about where it is anchored. After a training camp in Slovenia, the squad beat Konyaspor 3-0 and Çaykur Rizespor 2-1 and drew 1-1 with Kasımpaşa on a Turkish tour, before closing out pre-season in Europe with a 2-0 defeat to Eintracht Frankfurt and a goalless draw against Nice. On paper it reads like an ordinary summer schedule, but an English club spending a meaningful share of its pre-season in Türkiye is a small, telling sign of how deeply the owner's identity runs through the club's daily life.

The Opening: Discipline Beyond the Numbers

The picture sharpened quickly on their return to the Premier League. At home at the MKM Stadium, Hull City beat a Manchester United side with serious attacking talent 2-0. A week later they kept another clean sheet in a goalless draw with Aston Villa. The tally is there in black and white: three goals scored in three games, none conceded — one of the meanest defensive records in the division so far.

That is too consistent a pattern to put down to luck. Most promoted clubs lose their defensive shape in the opening weeks and get picked apart by top-flight attacks. Hull City did the opposite: they secured the back line first and added the attacking layers on top, patiently. Beating a side like Manchester United by two clear goals at home was the clearest evidence yet that the discipline is real.

What an Investment Looks Like on the Pitch

Success in football is usually measured in transfer spend. Hull City are a reminder of a different kind of success: institutional stability, built slowly, showing up in results. Since taking over, Acun Ilıcalı has repeated the same line — that this is a long-term project. The return on that message is currently written near the top of the Premier League table.

A note of caution is in order against early-season optimism, of course. Three matches is the first kilometre of a thirty-eight-week marathon. The real question is whether Hull City can hold this discipline as the division's heavyweights find their rhythm. But climbing this far up the Premier League without losing the club's identity, without alienating its supporters and without drowning in debt is an achievement worth marking on its own terms.

The FutbolRadar Read

Hull City's early-season table is not just a good start by an English club; it is the most tangible proof yet of a football project built abroad by a Turkish entrepreneur. The line that runs from restoring the crest to building a defensive structure shows what patience and sound planning can be worth on the pitch. The Premier League standings are worth watching closely over the coming weeks — because Hull City staying this close to the summit is shaping up to be one of the stories of the season.

For a side that beat Manchester United 2-0 at home and kept another clean sheet against Aston Villa, the question is no longer "is this a fluke?" but "how many more weeks can this last?"
