Liverpool Win but Don't Keep It Tight: The Opening Fenerbahçe Are Looking for at Kadıköy

FutbolRadar 04 November 2026

9 points but 7 goals conceded, 1 win in 8 games on Turkish soil and a former Liverpool man on the touchline: the numbers of the Kadıköy night.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Dear futbolradar.com readers, at 20:45 this evening at the Chobani Stadium, Fenerbahçe host Liverpool in matchday four of the Champions League league phase [4]. The two clubs are meeting for the first time; Fenerbahçe will be Liverpool's 140th different opponent in Europe [2][3]. On paper the picture is clear: Liverpool three from three, Fenerbahçe without a win. But in the favourites' numbers there is a line nobody is saying out loud: Liverpool win, but they do not keep it tight.

Table and form

The pre-match situation according to İHA data reported by Sabah [3]:

Liverpool: 3 games, 3 wins, 9 points, 3rd on goal difference

Fenerbahçe: 3 games, 2 draws, 1 defeat, 2 points, 31st

Fenerbahçe's results: Roma 1-1, Slavia Prague 2-2, Aston Villa 0-6

Liverpool's results: Atlético Madrid 2-1, LASK 6-3, Villarreal 4-3

Fenerbahçe have gone 10 games without a win in the Champions League group and league phases since beating Chelsea on 2 April 2008 [3]. They also arrive on the back of two straight 2-1 league defeats; the last was 1-2 to Göztepe [1]. And the man who took temporary charge of the team last week is Dirk Kuyt, who made 285 appearances in a Liverpool shirt [1][2].

Liverpool are not comfortable in the Premier League either: 5th on 15 points from 9 games, having lost to Manchester City and Arsenal [3]. They lost 1-0 to Arsenal on 1 November [1].

The favourites' hidden weakness

Liverpool have scored 12 goals in 3 games in Europe; this season only PSG have scored more [2]. But in the same 3 games they have conceded 7 [3]. They won the Atlético and Villarreal games from behind [3]. According to the club's own statistician, none of Liverpool's last 46 European games has ended in a draw: 32 wins, 14 defeats [2].

This is my reading: that is the profile not of a team in control, but of one that accepts goal traffic at both ends. For Fenerbahçe the task is not to stop Liverpool but to take their share in that traffic.

The second statistic is even more striking. Liverpool have won only once in 8 away games against Turkish teams, with 1 draw and 6 defeats. The irony is that the only win was away at Trabzonspor in 2010, and Kuyt scored the late goal in that game. All six defeats were by a single goal, the last three 1-0 [2].

To be fair, there is counter-evidence too: Liverpool have 7 wins and 2 defeats in their last 9 away games in the group and league phases of this competition [2]. So the Türkiye statistic is not destiny, but a warning. Iraola is not underestimating his opponents either; of the Fenerbahçe players he says: "They are experienced, they know how these kinds of games are played in this competition" [1].

Fenerbahçe's own history is less encouraging: 11 wins, 8 draws and 24 defeats in 43 Champions League games; 45 goals scored, 79 conceded [3].

Squad: both sides are carrying wounds

For Fenerbahçe, Nathan Ake and Marco Asensio are injured. Jayden Oosterwolde has not played since August, and Kojo Peprah Oppong's situation is uncertain [1]. So Kuyt has to build a depleted centre-back line. The XI that took the field against Göztepe was: Ederson, Semedo, Skriniar, Oppong, Mercan, Kante, Guendouzi, Aydın, Kahveci, Aktürkoğlu, Lukaku [1].

For Liverpool, Ryan Gravenberch is not in the travelling squad because of a heel fracture. Jeremie Frimpong is out with a muscle injury, and Conor Bradley, Hugo Ekitike and Giovanni Leoni with long-term injuries. Federico Chiesa and Wataru Endo were not registered for the league phase [1]. Asked about Trey Nyoni as a replacement for Gravenberch, Andoni Iraola said: "We trust him" [1].

Key duels

First duel: Fenerbahçe's patched-up centre-back line against Liverpool's attack. Liverpool's top scorers in Europe are Mac Allister and Barcola with 3 each [3]; Barcola scored a hat-trick on their last European trip, at LASK [2]. In the Premier League, Alexander Isak is the team's top scorer with 5 [3].

Second duel: midfield. Iraola's own words sound like a confession: "They have a very good technical level in midfield. If we give them too much time on the ball or don't press properly, we will suffer." And he adds: "The stronger we start, the fewer chances we give Fenerbahçe." [1]

This is my reading: in a Liverpool midfield without Gravenberch, Kante and Guendouzi breaking the first wave of the press is the real key to this game. If the first 20 minutes belong to Fenerbahçe, the scenario Iraola fears begins.

Referee: France's Clement Turpin. His assistants are Nicolas Danos and Benjamin Pages, the fourth official is Jeremy Stinat; Willy Delajod is on VAR and Eric Wattellier on AVAR [4][5].

Closing

At his press conference Kuyt said: "We really have good players, but we need to show up" [1]. For Fenerbahçe this game is also a threshold for breaking away from the bottom of the table: they are 31st on 2 points [3].

This is my reading: Liverpool are the favourites, no argument there. But against a team that has conceded 7 in 3 games and won only once in 8 trips to Türkiye, Fenerbahçe's chances depend less on their depleted defence than on their own courage to find goals. This is my reading: given the pattern of Liverpool's last 46 European games, a draw would be the least expected result.
