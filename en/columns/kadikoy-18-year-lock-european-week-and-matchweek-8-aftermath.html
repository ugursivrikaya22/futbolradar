An 18-Year Lock at Kadıköy: European Week and the Bill for Matchweek 8

FutbolRadar 20 October 2026

Opinion / Europe and the Süper Lig | What the numbers say: runs are there to be broken, but they don't break by themselves

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Full-time note: Fenerbahçe–Slavia Prague ended 2-2; Fenerbahçe's winless run in the Champions League has reached 10 games [9]. The match analysis: Fenerbahçe 2-2 Slavia Prague full-time analysis. The text below was written before the game.

Tonight at Kadıköy only one of two runs can be broken. Perhaps neither.

According to UEFA's pre-match data, Fenerbahçe have gone nine games without a win in the Champions League (three draws, six defeats). That is the club's longest winless run in the competition; their last win was 2-1 against Chelsea in the first leg of the 2007/08 quarter-final. Their opponents Slavia Prague have an even longer run: no win in 21 games; they last beat Steaua in September 2007 and have never won away in the competition [3].

Kadıköy: 1 point, 6 goals and a question

Fenerbahçe collected only one point in the first two matchdays; that point came from a 1-1 draw with Roma at home [1]. Then came a heavy 6-0 defeat at Villa Park. Slavia arrive without a point after losing 3-2 to Lens and 2-0 to Sabah [2]. So on paper, tonight is a game neither team "can afford to lose".

But the really striking number is another one: according to UEFA, Fenerbahçe have not led at half-time in any of their last 12 Champions League games. On the other hand, they have never lost a home Champions League game in which they scored first (seven wins, one draw). Slavia have never led at half-time away from home and have never won a game in which they conceded first [3].

Put those two statistics side by side and the key to the game reveals itself: the first goal. For İsmail Kartal's team, who regained morale by beating Alanyaspor 3-0 after the Aston Villa defeat [2], the issue is not controlling the game but turning control into a scoreline early. On a night when Mert Müldür and Marco Asensio are missing [2], it is also a test of squad depth.

One more note: UEFA points out that Romelu Lukaku has produced two goals and two assists in his last three games against Slavia [3]. The match page: Fenerbahçe - Slavia Prague. What we wrote about the Roma night still applies: An unfinished Champions League night.

Lille: Galatasaray's France problem

For Galatasaray, who visit Lille tomorrow at 19:45, the picture is different but no less uncomfortable. UEFA's data: Galatasaray have not won in their last six Champions League league-phase games (one draw, five defeats) and have lost their last five away games in the competition. They have also gone six games without a win in France [3].

Lille's numbers cut both ways: they are unbeaten in six European games against Turkish opponents, and they have scored first in their last eight home Champions League games. But they have lost their last four European games and have failed to keep a clean sheet in their last ten Champions League games [3]. Lille conceding looks almost certain; the question is whether Galatasaray can hold on in the game without conceding first.

The story has an emotional side too: Victor Osimhen scored 18 goals in 38 games for Lille in 2019/20, and Rafael Leão spent 2018/19 there [3]. Match page: Lille - Galatasaray. For the overall European picture: Champions League standings.

Matchweek 8: the top tightened, Trabzon made the difference

Matchweek 8 of the Süper Lig closed last night at Papara Park. Trabzonspor beat Beşiktaş 2-0 with goals from Ozan Tufan (21') and Mohamed Salah (71') [4][6].

The resulting picture: leaders Galatasaray came back from Gençlerbirliği with a 1-0 win through Deniz Gül's goal, moving to 19 points. Fenerbahçe are third on 16 points, Trabzonspor fourth on 16, Beşiktaş fifth on 15 [4]. So there are only a few points between second and fifth; a single matchweek could change the order completely. Current table: Süper Lig standings.

On Trabzonspor's side the number is striking: according to NTV, the team have not conceded a goal in three league games under Thomas Reis; two of them were against Galatasaray and Beşiktaş, and six goals were scored in those two games [5]. For Beşiktaş, Papara Park has now become a habit problem: the black-and-whites have not won in their last six games against Trabzonspor at this stadium (four draws, two defeats) [5]. The derby preview is here: Trabzonspor-Beşiktaş preview.

The transfer front: a FIFA ban for Eyüpspor

The most concrete transfer development this week is not a signing but a ban. Eyüpspor appeared on FIFA's registration ban list: the club were given a three-window transfer ban, starting on 15 October 2026 [7][8].

This could mean a team fighting in the lower reaches of the table entering the January transfer window without the option of strengthening the squad. An official statement from the club on the conditions for lifting the ban will determine the real limits of January planning.

Final word

All of this week's numbers say the same thing: runs are there to be broken, but they don't break by themselves. Fenerbahçe's 18-year lock, Galatasaray's away run and Beşiktaş's Papara Park problem all await a tactical answer. Tonight at Kadıköy, whoever scores first will start writing that answer.
