This Isn't Good Enough for Galatasaray: A Point Rescued in the 90th Minute Against Çorum

FutbolRadar 14 August 2026

Trendyol Süper Lig 2026-27, Matchday 1 | Galatasaray 2-2 Arca Çorum FK | RAMS Park, 14 August 2026

Share this column
fShare on Facebook XShare on X

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

When Gabriel Sara's cross hung in the air in the 90th minute, RAMS Park rose as one body. Victor Osimhen climbed above the defence, the ball hit the net, and the stadium erupted. But something was missing in that noise. It wasn't the sound of a win. It was the sound of relief. And when a Galatasaray supporter exhales in relief at home, on the opening night of the season, against a club playing its first ever top-flight match, against a side that had been down to ten men for twenty minutes — there is nothing to celebrate. There are only questions to ask.

I'm asking them. Because supporting Galatasaray means understanding that even joy has a standard. In our part of the world a last-minute goal can feel like a festival — but a last-minute equaliser against Çorum isn't a festival. It's a warning. A team that has won four consecutive league titles should not open its season by salvaging a scoreline. Honestly, three points weren't even the whole demand. The demand was to look at the eleven men on that pitch and say, "that's Galatasaray." Across ninety minutes, not once could we say it.

The first half: dominant, but unfinished

Let's be fair. Galatasaray owned the opening forty-five. Osimhen headed past the goalkeeper on 13 minutes only for a defender to clear off the line. On 15 he rose again to Barış Alper's cross and put it inches wide of the post. On 25 his low effort was pushed away by Marcos Felipe at full stretch. Yunus Akgün had a header ruled out for offside. Sara tested the keeper from range and struck the wall from a free kick.

The chances were there. The intent was there. But the half ended goalless, and that goalless half was the first instalment on a bill that came due after the break. Big teams convert the thirty minutes they dominate — because if they don't, the opponent walks into the dressing room believing something is possible. Çorum walked in with exactly that belief.

Eight minutes: from 53 to 61

Osimhen forced another corner from Leroy Sané's cross early in the second half, and on 53 minutes Torreira threaded him in behind, he rounded Felipe and finished. 1-0. The stadium relaxed. Everyone assumed the floodgates were about to open.

Six minutes later Alexandros Kyziridis struck a superb effort from distance: 1-1. Two minutes after that, Davinson Sánchez's back pass came up short, Jesús Ramírez pounced, and it was 1-2.

Eight minutes. The one thing this team needed most at the start of a long season — control — evaporated in eight minutes. And the criticism here has to be made with its reasoning attached rather than aimed at a person: Sánchez's mistake was an individual error, and individual errors happen. The real question is why that error turned into a goal so easily, and why the team then conceded again almost immediately. A side leading at home does not concede twice in two minutes because of one player. It concedes because the reflex to regroup after the first goal simply didn't fire — because the midfield couldn't hold the ball, kill the tempo, or slow the game down. For a squad that intends to survive a long domestic campaign and a European one alongside it, that is the most alarming gap on show.

Twenty minutes against ten men

Kyziridis was sent off on 70 minutes for a challenge on Yunus Akgün. Galatasaray had twenty minutes and an extra man.

What followed was not a champion overwhelming a promoted side. It was sideways passing, backwards passing, crosses launched hopefully into the box, and a lot of hope. The equaliser arrived in the 90th, and it arrived from another cross and another header. The Plan B was really just Plan A repeated louder.

That is where the quality gap lives. The difference in budget, squad depth and experience between the two clubs is enormous and beyond argument. But in football that gap doesn't project itself onto the pitch — it has to be imposed. It wasn't. Çorum looked brave, organised and physically ready in their first ever Süper Lig fixture, and they went home with their heads high. Acknowledging that doesn't weaken the criticism of Galatasaray. It sharpens it.

Why winning alone isn't enough

Someone will say: relax, it's matchday one, you got a point. Fine. But this support's relationship with football was never built on glancing at a scoreboard and feeling satisfied. This terrace remembers how matches were won far longer than it remembers that they were. Galatasaray's rivals aren't only Fenerbahçe and Beşiktaş — they are Galatasaray's own history. This shirt is supposed to make the opposition feel, from the first whistle, that they're in for a difficult evening. That feeling comes before the trophies, and it's what brings the trophies.

Çorum never felt it. They didn't collapse after conceding, didn't shrink after going ahead, didn't panic after the red card. When an opponent is that comfortable against you, the problem isn't your scoreline. It's your appearance.

The manager's choices

Okan Buruk said it himself before kick-off: the bench was short of attacking options. That was both an observation and an admission. With names like İlkay Gündoğan, Wilfried Singo, Mario Lemina, Eren Elmalı and new arrival Lesley Ugochukwu available, the genuine game-changers for a side trailing 1-2 were limited.

Buruk's league record speaks for itself, and his numbers against newly promoted teams are outstanding. Which is precisely why last night should be read as a signal rather than an accident. A team that won only one of five pre-season friendlies — beaten by Monza and Venezia, held 3-3 by Rennes — and is still absorbing players returning from a World Cup summer was always likely to look like this in week one. The transfer window is still open. For ninety minutes, the areas of this squad that still need finishing made themselves impossible to ignore.

Who stood up, who fell short

Victor Osimhen carried his team to a point on his own. Two goals, endless aerial duels, relentless running. You can't help but applaud him. Barış Alper Yılmaz kept trying to create down the left and set up the first half's most dangerous moments. Gabriel Sara took responsibility, both with efforts from distance and with the assist for the equaliser.

Leroy Sané's decisive moments never stretched across the full ninety. The defensive line was unconvincing on concentration and positioning for both goals. And the midfield's ability to cool a game down was nowhere to be found in the ten minutes that decided it.

One match doesn't define a season — but it does send a signal

Now the measured part, because there's a difference between anger and losing your head.

This is one game. Matchday one. The squad is unfinished. Post-World-Cup adaptation problems are not unique to Galatasaray. This same team could look entirely different in three weeks, and probably will. Declaring the season over on the back of a draw would mean losing in the stands the exact composure that went missing on the pitch.

But this also needs saying: Galatasaray's target was never to beat Çorum. Galatasaray's target is to look ready for Europe even while beating Çorum. This club's bar isn't a point rescued on the opening weekend. It's a fifth consecutive title and standing tall in the Champions League. Measured against that bar, last night wasn't a disaster — but it was unmistakably a warning.

Osimhen's 90th-minute header rescued a point. But Galatasaray was never made great by rescued points — it was made great by matches that never needed rescuing.
