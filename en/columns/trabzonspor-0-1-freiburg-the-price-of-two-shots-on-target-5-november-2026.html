Trabzonspor 0-1 Freiburg: 63 Per Cent of the Ball, and the Price of Two Shots on Target

FutbolRadar 05 November 2026

The ball was with Trabzonspor, the accuracy with Freiburg: the attacking test told by a 0-1.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Trabzonspor 0-1 Freiburg: on the evening of 5 November 2026 at Papara Park, in matchday three of the UEFA Conference League league phase, the winner was decided by Lukas Kübler's 14th-minute goal. In the game, refereed by Kristo Tohver and kicked off at 20:45, the claret-and-blues suffered their first defeat of the league phase; both teams have now reached six points.[1][2][4]

This is my reading: the objection tonight should not be to the score, but to the easy assumption that possession equals control of the game. 63 per cent of the ball, only two shots on target: Trabzonspor's six-point European identity kept its fight in the face of absentees, but failed the test of sustained creativity. That is exactly where the real debate begins, between the quantity of the ball and the quality of the threat.

The second ball from a corner, the only goal of the night

In the 14th minute, from Grifo's corner on the left, Matanović headed at goal and Onana dived to his right to save; Kübler, inside the six-yard box, finished the rebound. Onana's first intervention was not enough to stop the goal.[2] Onana also gathered Matanović's 21st-minute shot after it deflected off the defence, and saved Kübler's free kick in the 39th.[2]

For Trabzonspor, Muçi's shot from distance in the 29th minute went wide; in the 41st Salah controlled Saviolo's cross and shot over.[2] BBC's record has yellow cards for Saviolo (27) and Nwaiwu (53), and for Freiburg, Eggestein (68), Engelhardt (81) and Makengo (84).[1] In the closing stages Nwaiwu headed over in the 85th; at 90+2 Backhaus saved Salah's shot from inside the box, and the game ended at 90+4.[1]

This is my reading: closing the book on the goal as just a set-piece defending error would be incomplete; the sharing of space after the first save is part of defending too. That the ball the goalkeeper pushed out became a chance for the opposition full-back is a reminder that the defensive job does not end after the first contact. Salah's chance in stoppage time shows a draw was still possible; but loading the explanation for the whole night onto a single save hides the question of why the earlier attacks came to nothing.

Only two of 15 shots hit the target

The statistics, Trabzonspor first and Freiburg second:[1][3]

Possession: 63%–37%. Shots: 15–12. Shots on target: 2–5.[1]

Corners: 7–4. Goalkeeper saves: 4–2. Touches in the opposition box: 27–17.[1]

Expected goals (xG), FotMob: 1.67–0.99.[3]

BBC also recorded 31–18 crosses, 572–335 passes and 149–43 successful passes in the final third.[1] Six of Trabzonspor's 15 shots came from outside the box; eight missed the target.[1]

This is my reading: these numbers call not for banning the pass but for discussing where the pass ends up. More touches in the box and a higher xG refute the verdict that the home side produced nothing; two shots on target, though, question how well that production was directed at goal. An xG advantage is not an automatic certificate of points dropped. FotMob's total describes the accumulation of chances, the score describes what actually happened; the two need to be read without substituting one for the other. While Freiburg found five shots on target with less of the ball, Trabzonspor's extra passes did not turn into goalkeeper saves to the same degree.

The absentees are not an excuse but the measure of the game plan

Cabral was suspended; Aral Şimşir, Franculino, Batagov and Onuralp Çevikkan were out injured.[4] Before the game Thomas Reis had said: "If we count attacking players, maybe we only have 3-4 players available."[5] That was not a post-match explanation but an assessment of the squad before the game.

In BBC's starting XI, the front three were Salah–Onuachu–Saviolo and the midfield Mendy–Fabinho–Muçi. Reis brought on Ozan Tufan for Mendy in the 58th minute and Bouchouari for Mustafa Eskihellaç in the 87th.[1]

This is my reading: the answer to the key pre-match question is partly negative: without Cabral and Franculino, the attacking output of the six-point start could not be carried forward. But we cannot declare which absence broke which position without data; what we have is not a hypothesis about missing players but two shots on target from the side that played. Seeing Onuachu's presence alongside 31 crosses does not mean the high ball was the attack's only explanation; even so, in his next work Reis should question not so much the number of crosses as whether they turn into finishable chances. Before the list of missing names, the connections between the remaining ones should be discussed.

Six points stand, the perfect start is over

After 4-2 away at KuPS and 2-0 against Hearts, Trabzonspor lost their third game.[4][2] In BBC's provisional table at full time the claret-and-blues were eighth on six points, Freiburg eleventh on the same points; some teams had played only two games.[1] This ranking is not the final matchday-three table after all of the night's games were completed. For the current picture, follow the Conference League standings.

The next stop on the calendar is away at Alanyaspor on Sunday 8 November at 18:30 Turkish time.[7] In Europe there is a trip to CSKA Sofia on 26 November.[6] The club's overall picture is on the Trabzonspor page, and the league table on Trabzonspor standings.

This is my reading: this defeat does not erase the six points, but it shows they are no guarantee. Trabzonspor keeping their European identity depends, before asking for more of the ball, on directing that ball at goal more often; that is the most concrete demand Papara Park will take from this night.
