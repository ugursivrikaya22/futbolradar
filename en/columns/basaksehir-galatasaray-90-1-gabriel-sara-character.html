That Goal in 90+1: Galatasaray's Character Spoke at Başakşehir

FutbolRadar 04 September 2026

Trendyol Süper Lig 2026-27, Matchday 4 | Başakşehir 2-3 Galatasaray | Başakşehir Fatih Terim Stadium, 4 September 2026

The scoreboard changed in the 91st minute, and the 2-3 written on it will look like the only thing worth discussing all week: Galatasaray won, came away with three points, the pursuit of top spot goes on. But the real story out of the Başakşehir Fatih Terim Stadium is not that comfortable. This was an evening on which a team could not hold its superiority, lost the advantage twice after taking it twice, and in the end won the match not through tactical control but through the flash of an individual moment.

Galatasaray have not given the appearance of a comfortable win in any match up to matchweek four this season. A point rescued in the 90th minute against Çorum, then wins over Erzurumspor and Göztepe, and now another last-minute drama at Başakşehir. That is not a coincidence; it is a pattern. And patterns tell you more than single matches.

The first half-hour: masters of the pitch, not of the scoreline

Osimhen put Galatasaray ahead on 17 minutes. From a move that developed as Sané controlled and turned with a ball from the right, the Nigerian sent it into the net with the cool finishing he has made a habit of this season. Up to that minute, and for twenty minutes after it, there was only one team on the pitch. Galatasaray had the ball, penned Başakşehir into their own half and created at least three clear chances for a second goal.

They did not find it. And that is the first thing that needs discussing afterwards: a team that fails to score a second in thirty minutes of dominance while 1-0 up is handing its opponent hope of survival. We saw the same script against Çorum, and in the Rennes friendly. When superiority is not converted into a scoreline, the match always comes cheaper for the opposition.

The 66th minute: a penalty and a shift in momentum

Başakşehir clung on after the 45th minute through the high press they applied the moment they lost the ball in defence. Shomurodov converted the penalty awarded in the box on 66 minutes with real composure: 1-1. Immediately after that goal the pitch stretched, and Galatasaray's midfield lost the control it had held in the first half.

Galatasaray's answer came quickly. On 71 minutes Lucas Torreira found space outside the box and drove the ball into the net: 1-2. With that goal the Uruguayan showed both his own scoring contribution and his team's capacity to regroup after the break. At that moment the match looked to be back under control.

The 76th minute: same error, different outcome

It was not. From a corner won on the Başakşehir right, Skov Olsen's delivery sailed over goalkeeper Emin Bayram, struck Davinson Sánchez at the near post and went in: 2-2. The question of concentration in the defensive line, discussed after the Çorum match, reappeared four weeks later with the same name attached and again from an aerial situation. That is not an error repeating by chance; it is a weakness that needs solving.

You would expect a team pegged back to 2-2 by an own goal away from home to come apart psychologically. Galatasaray did not — and that is where the real narrative of this match begins.

90+1: the minute character spoke

As stoppage time arrived, everybody was bracing for a draw. Right then Gabriel Sara played the role of last-minute hero he has taken on all season once more and swept the ball home to win Galatasaray the three points: 2-3. After his assist in the Çorum match, this time he finished the job himself.

A player deciding the outcome of a match for the second time since the opening weekend deserves the praise. But before presenting that as a team virtue, honesty is required: a team winning its matches in the 91st minute does not only show that it has character, it also shows that it has not finished the job across ninety minutes. Both things can be true at once. That is precisely why Galatasaray deserve both praise and scrutiny this season.

Why this is a warning rather than a celebration

In four different matches across four weeks, Galatasaray have failed to translate their superiority fully into the scoreline and have left the job to the closing minutes every time. The opponent here was Başakşehir, a mid-table Süper Lig side at least so far this season; a penalty in the box and an own goal exposed the cracks in the visitors' defensive organisation.

Title races are won not by a four-point margin but by ninety minutes of discipline. This team cannot rely on Osimhen's finishing, Torreira's midfield contribution and Sara's late reflexes for a whole season. On the day those three go quiet, what is left is a defensive line that establishes superiority in the first half-hour and cannot protect it.

Standouts

Victor Osimhen put his team ahead with another goal and sustained his pressing for ninety minutes. Lucas Torreira was one of the most balanced performers on the pitch, both for his goal and for his organising role in midfield. Gabriel Sara wrote himself in as the match-winner once again with his late goal. Davinson Sánchez's own goal, on the other hand, showed once more that the concentration problem in defence has still not been solved.

Conclusion: three points, no peace of mind

Galatasaray came away from Başakşehir with three points, which was the result they were looking for on the scoreboard. But the lesson supporters should take from this win is not the comfort of "we win anyway" — it is the opposite: this team has still not learned to close out the superiority it establishes, and it carries a recurring pattern of errors at the back. As the opposition weakens over the rest of the season, those gaps can be papered over. But in Europe, and in matches against the sides at the top of the league, a win that waits until the ninety-first minute will not always arrive.
