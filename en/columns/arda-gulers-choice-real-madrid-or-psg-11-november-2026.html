Arda Güler's Choice

FutbolRadar 11 November 2026

The claimed gap between Real Madrid's new contract offer and PSG's salary proposal is the talk of the week. Nothing is official, but both sides of the scales now speak in numbers.

Going makes sense. Staying would be beautiful. In football, those two are not always the same thing.

FutbolRadar

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Two numbers tonight, and nothing else to begin with: 8 and 22. According to a claim doing the rounds on social media and a handful of websites in recent days, Real Madrid offered Arda Güler a new contract worth €8 million a year, and Arda has not signed it. At the other end of the table sits Paris Saint-Germain, offering, by the same claim, a salary of €22 million a year. Let me underline this: neither Real Madrid, nor PSG, nor the player's camp has confirmed these figures, and nobody has even said whether they are net or gross. Worth remembering: an earlier claim, attributed to L'Équipe, spoke of a net €18 million offer from PSG [1], so this is not the first time Paris has been said to be playing big on salary. These numbers now sit at the very centre of the debate. So, the maths first.

Paris's figure is 2.75 times Madrid's. Assume a five-year deal and the gap comes to €70 million: 70 per cent of the €100 million market value our own market-value list puts on Arda. Now look in the rear-view mirror. Last October, Fanatik reported that Arda was earning €2.6 million net a year at Madrid and that the club was preparing to raise it to €4.5 million [4]. From 2.6 to 8, from 8 to 22: that line is less a portrait of one player than a chart of the appetite for him.

The transfer-fee side has not been quiet either. In August, PSG were reported to have offered Madrid €115 million [3]; last week came the claim that the figure could rise to €200 million [1]. Fenerbahçe's 20 per cent sell-on clause is part of the sums too: a €200 million sale could leave €40 million in the club's account [1]. Arda's contract runs until 2029 [3]. So Madrid are not the side in a hurry; Madrid have time. The only person whose clock is ticking is a 21-year-old entering the most productive years of his career.

A career plan at twenty-one

We know Arda's story by heart. In the summer of 2023 he left Fenerbahçe for Madrid in a deal reported as €20 million plus €10 million, and signed for six years [7]. He was 18. Then came the injuries, the evenings on the bench, the endless "why isn't Arda playing" debates. And then last Saturday at Mestalla: Valencia 0-3 Real Madrid, with Arda scoring in the 36th minute and setting up Mbappé's goal in the 75th [5]. The match file from that night is here. He is 21 now, and what we call a career plan is no longer a dream. It is a calendar.

A player's plan at this age rests on three questions. Let me try to ask them on Arda's behalf.

First: what role will I play? According to Sözcü today, Arda has not yet decided on a new contract; he does not want to sign before he sees what role José Mourinho has in mind for him [2]. That sentence says it all. At Madrid, Arda is still a project for the future; in Paris, Luis Enrique is reported to have wanted him for a long time [3]. One voice says "be patient, your turn will come". The other says "it is your turn". At 21, there is no need to say which one sounds sweeter.

Second: a leap to a European giant, or a move between giants? Arda already plays for a giant, so the real question is not "going to a big club". It is which big club puts him at its centre. In May, in Budapest, PSG beat Arsenal on penalties after a 1-1 draw to win the Champions League for the second season running, and Luis Enrique lifted the trophy for the third time as a coach [6]. This is not a club telling Arda "come and grow with us"; it is a club saying "we are already at the top, come and keep us there". Two years ago Kylian Mbappé walked from Paris to Madrid [8]. Now Paris has its eye on Madrid's brightest young left foot. Sometimes football writes its own jokes.

Third: which league do I want to play in? This is where romance and reality part ways. La Liga is a test every week: Mestalla, the Metropolitano, San Mamés, and the never-ending race at the top of the table. In Ligue 1, PSG spend many weekends against opponents who retreat into their own box; the real exams come on Tuesday and Wednesday nights in Europe. On one side, a league that makes you sweat every Sunday; on the other, a calendar that turns Sundays into warm-ups and Tuesdays into finals. Arda's game shines in tight spaces, between crowded defences. On paper, Ligue 1 promises that game more of the ball, more space and more minutes. But a soft league can also blunt a sharp foot.

The case for going

Let me be straight: if Arda chooses to go, I will not be the first to blame him. In fact, I will defend him.

Money is the language of respect. In football, a salary is not just a bank balance; it is a club announcing where it sees you. The club that says 22 is saying "you are the pillar of this team". The club that says 8 is saying "we love you, but it is still someone else's turn". If the claimed figures are right, the €14 million gap is the price tag on a difference in status.

The career is short, the window is narrow. Between 21 and 29, a footballer is at his peak. Spending two or three of those years waiting "for my role to become clear" is a loss no contract clause can give back. At 18, Arda already found the nerve to leave Istanbul for Madrid. Getting up and moving once more would not betray that boy's story. It would continue it.

Being the centre of a champion team. Joining a side that has won two Champions Leagues in a row as "one of the stars" makes you grow far faster than waiting your turn in a squad full of stars. A team built around you is a different school altogether. Arda's left foot is good enough to carry that weight; the night at Mestalla showed it once again.

And yet...

And yet this is where the pen stops. Because some things never fit in a spreadsheet.

Two years ago, when Mbappé moved from Paris to Madrid, he was reported to have accepted a drop from €32 million net a year to €26 million [8]. Some players go to Madrid for less money, because the white shirt at the Bernabéu has a price that no payroll ever shows. A Turkish kid running in that shirt is not just a footballer; he is Turkish football's window on the world. And that goal at Mestalla was a sign that the door is opening right now.

Madrid are not silent either. According to Sözcü, the club is considering extending Arda's contract to 2035, raising his salary and adding an €800 million release clause, though no official offer has been made yet [2]. In September, according to a report by AS, the two sides had shaken hands on a new deal running to 2032, but the signature never came [9]. There is no louder way for a club to say "we want you for a decade" than an €800 million clause.

FutbolRadar's reading

This is not a transfer story, and there is no signed decision on the table. What we have is a pair of scales. On one side: 22 million, the centre of a champion team, and a career calculation that justifies impatience. On the other: 8 million, a white shirt, an answer Mourinho has not yet given, and an inner voice saying "maybe the best of it is right here".

Going makes sense. Staying would be beautiful. In football, those two are not always the same thing. If Arda picks Paris, I will stand behind him; I have made the case for it above, and I would make it again. But I also know that on the night he scores his first goal at the Parc des Princes, I will look for that white shirt on the television screen for a moment, just a moment. And I know the sentence that will run through my head: I wish he had stayed.
