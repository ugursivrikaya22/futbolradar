Beşiktaş Back on Their Feet in Europe: The Hoffenheim Win Is More Than a Result

FutbolRadar 17 October 2026

Hoffenheim 2-3 Beşiktaş. Six points from two games: the black-and-whites have made a strong start; now all eyes are on the Crystal Palace test.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Translated from the Turkish original: Read the original column in Turkish

Being a big team in Europe is not measured only by making big signings. Sometimes three points won away from home, sometimes resilience shown in difficult moments, and sometimes the confidence carried into the next game define a team's identity.

When Beşiktaş came back from Hoffenheim with a 3-2 win on 15 October, they didn't just win three points. Having beaten Marseille 4-1 in the first round of the UEFA Europa League, the black-and-whites took their tally to six points after the second game. [1]

From Marseille to Hoffenheim: coincidence or transformation?

First a 4-1 win over Marseille, then a 3-2 victory over Hoffenheim in Germany. Two games, six points. Looking at that picture, it is hard not to feel hopeful about Beşiktaş's European journey.

But the real issue is not just the table. Beating two opponents with different football cultures is an important sign that the black-and-whites can compete at this level. Still, two games do not prove that every problem has been solved.

Big European teams are not content to be heroes for a night; they repeat the right playing habits.

Three goals are encouraging, two conceded are a concern

Scoring three away from home is valuable. But conceding two in the same game does not end the debate about defensive security and game control.

The basic question facing Beşiktaş is this: while keeping their attacking productivity, how far will their defensive organisation be improved?

In a fixture list that includes Crystal Palace, Celtic and Bayer Leverkusen, positioning after losing the ball and the speed of the transition to defence may become even more important. Here we are not talking about a definitive tactical diagnosis of a particular incident, but about a general need for development that the result has revealed.

The Crystal Palace game is no ordinary third round

On 22 October Beşiktaş host Crystal Palace. [1] For a team that has collected six points in two weeks, this game is an important test of how rising expectations will be managed.

If Beşiktaş win, they will finish the first three rounds with nine points. That could create a valuable advantage in terms of the aim of finishing in the top eight of the league phase and going straight through to the round of 16; but on its own it would not guarantee qualification. [2]

Support from the stands will matter, but it will not be enough on its own. The black-and-whites need to produce a controlled, patient game that maintains tactical discipline.

What do Beşiktaş fans want to see on European nights?

Beşiktaş fans care not just about the score on the board but about a fight that reflects the weight of the shirt. The wins over Marseille and Hoffenheim have naturally raised expectations.

What will decide whether this excitement proves temporary is the consistency of the play and results produced in the games to come.

Final word: the real story starts now

If Beşiktaş want to have a say in Europe, they have made the right start. But success will be decided not by individual nights but by consistency across the whole season.

The Hoffenheim win is a strong sign; the Crystal Palace game will show how sustainable that sign is.

FutbolRadar's question: can Beşiktaş finish in the Europa League's top eight this season?
