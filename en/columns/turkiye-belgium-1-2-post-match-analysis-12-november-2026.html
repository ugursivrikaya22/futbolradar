Seven Minutes in Front: Türkiye 1-2 Belgium and Sergen Yalçın's First Lesson

FutbolRadar 12 November 2026

12 November 2026 · İzmir, Gürsel Aksel Stadium · UEFA Nations League A, Group 1, matchday 5. Türkiye 1-2 Belgium. Goals: 20' Arda Güler; 27' De Bruyne, 30' De Ketelaere. Next: Sunday 15 November, 22:45 Turkish time, France–Türkiye.

Our total time in front in this Nations League campaign is seven minutes. At the end of those seven minutes, we conceded twice in three.

Update · 12 November 2026 at 23:15

Post-match: Sergen Yalçın said each side had spells on top, that Türkiye had very clear chances in both halves and could have taken a point, and that he was satisfied with the effort. He named the team’s main problem as sustaining it: they need to compete over longer stretches rather than in bursts, and he expects that to improve over time. The sentence closest to this column’s “seven minutes” argument came from the coach himself. [10]

Until tonight, the time Türkiye had spent in front in this Nations League campaign was zero minutes. Across 360 minutes against France, Italy twice and Belgium in Liège, the national team had not led once. In this morning's numbers column we said the first goal in İzmir would be worth more than the scoreline. It came: in the 20th minute Arda Güler struck a free kick from outside the box straight into the net, and a packed Gürsel Aksel rose to its feet. [1] [2] [5]

The lead lasted seven minutes. In the 27th minute Jérémy Doku crossed from the left, Kevin De Bruyne was unmarked on the penalty spot, and it was level. Three minutes later the ball came back to De Cuyper after a Moreira attempt, and Charles De Ketelaere pushed it into an empty net: 1-2. [1] [2] [3] The score did not change in the remaining hour. Sergen Yalçın's first game in charge ended in defeat, and Türkiye were left without a point after a fifth match. [1] [4]

The game was lost between 27 and 30

If this defeat needs one sentence, it is this: Türkiye did not lose the game when they conceded, they lost it in the ten minutes after they scored. A lead buys time. You make the opponent open up, you set the tempo, you move the ball without hurry. None of that happened in İzmir. Belgium did not stop after levelling; three minutes later they scored again.

This was not a fluke but a repeat. Before kick-off we wrote that the first thing to watch in İzmir was not whether Türkiye scored first, but whether a second goal would follow the first one they conceded. In Bologna, after Aral Şimşir made it 1-1, Italy were back in front three minutes later. [7] In İzmir the film opened on a different scene but broke at the same point: a team that cannot absorb the first shock.

A forced choice at right-back, Doku on the left

On matchday Sergen Yalçın said Oğuz Aydın would start at right-back and Barış Alper Yılmaz up front. [6] Oğuz at full-back was not a preference but a necessity: Zeki Çelik was not fully fit. [2] [4] The cost of turning a winger into a full-back is easiest to read on that side. The cross for the equaliser came from Doku, on the flank facing Türkiye's right-back. [2] [3] Pinning that on one player would be unfair, but it is plainly the first thing the new coach has to fix.

The rest of the first half also shows Belgium doing a lot with little. Apart from Arda's free kick, the effort that troubled Lammens most before the break was Orkun Kökçü's in the 26th minute. Belgium kept threatening after their two goals: in the 38th minute Moreira's shot went just wide of the post. [1] By the time the half ended 1-2, Türkiye had already lost the most important ten minutes of the match.

Second half: Lammens' night

After the break Türkiye created more but could not beat Senne Lammens. He kept out Arda's shot in the 53rd minute, Barış Alper's effort in the 60th, and Deniz Gül's strike from Hakan Çalhanoğlu's pass in the 82nd. In the 92nd minute Hakan's shot, the closest Türkiye came to an equaliser, was stopped too. [1] [2] [3] At the other end Uğurcan Çakır saved De Bruyne's free kick in the 47th minute and De Ketelaere's attempt in the 59th to keep the gap at one. [1] [2] [3]

Yalçın moved in the 58th minute. Orkun Kökçü and Enes Ünal went off; Hakan Çalhanoğlu and Deniz Gül came on, and the captain's armband passed from Merih Demiral to Hakan. Zeki Çelik, whom he had said could manage 25-30 minutes, came on with Salih Özcan in the 70th minute. [1] [5] [6] The changes raised the pressure; Deniz Gül's chance came out of it. But second-half pressure was not enough to win back the advantage thrown away before the break.

In the 76th minute Arda Güler went down in the box. Referee Sandro Schärer waved play on and there was no call from VAR. [1] [3] That decision will be argued over. But the story of this match was not written in the 76th minute; it was written between 27 and 30.

Fourth place is settled, the sums go to Bordeaux

The result takes Belgium to 9 points. Türkiye have no points from five games, with three goals scored and thirteen conceded. [1] [4] [7] Even a win in France on Sunday would only take Türkiye to 3 points, so they can no longer catch Italy, who have at least 7, or Belgium. Fourth place in the group is mathematically certain. [7] All of the team's results are on our Türkiye national team page, and the Sergen Yalçın preview sets out what was at stake.

What that means is set by UEFA's 2026/27 rules. The four fourth-placed teams are ranked against each other. The bottom two go straight down to League B; the other two face League B group runners-up in the A/B play-offs. [8] [9] A fourth place with no points and a goal difference of minus ten sits very close to the bottom of that ranking. It is hard to see Türkiye holding on in that calculation without a point from Bordeaux. And the outcome no longer depends on Türkiye alone; it also depends on the fourth-placed teams in the other three groups.

FutbolRadar's take

It would be unfair to judge Sergen Yalçın on one game; this was the first match of his first camp. But the first night showed clearly which job is most urgent. This squad can score through Arda's left foot, can raise the pressure when Hakan comes on, and can lean on Uğurcan to stay in games. What it has not found is a way to carry a lead. Türkiye's total time in front in this Nations League campaign is seven minutes. At the end of those seven minutes, Belgium scored twice in three.

İzmir filled its stands and gave this team a chance; the team held on to it for seven minutes. [2] [5] On Sunday night it is France in Bordeaux. What is needed there is not a beautiful goal, but a team that can stay on its feet for the ten minutes after it scores or concedes. Those ten minutes will have the last word on Türkiye's place in League A.
