Fenerbahçe vs Slavia Praha: Three Plans Against the Press on a Must-Win Kadıköy Night

FutbolRadar 20 October 2026

Kadıköy'de 19.45'te: taktik, eksikler, form ve Fenerbahçe'nin 'ilk 24' hesabında bu maçın yeri.

This is an opinion column. It reflects the writer's personal view and should not be confused with FutbolRadar's verified match data.

Post-match note: This column was written before kick-off. Fenerbahçe drew 2-2 with Slavia Praha, conceding the equaliser in the 90th minute [10]. Our post-match analysis (in Turkish): Fenerbahçe 2-2 Slavia Prag.

Kadıköy hosts more than a Champions League fixture tonight. Fenerbahçe and Slavia Praha meet at 19:45 Istanbul time (18:45 CET) at the Chobani Stadium, with Spain's Ricardo de Burgos refereeing [1]. Neither side has won in the league phase yet: Fenerbahçe have one point, Slavia none [1][2]. We have already covered the long winless runs and the weight of the first goal; this column goes inside the match itself — how both teams will play, who is missing, and what these 90 minutes mean for Fenerbahçe's European plan. Match page: Fenerbahçe vs Slavia Praha.

What kind of opponent: tempo, man-marking and a back three

İsmail Kartal described Slavia plainly at his pre-match press conference: a team that is "very physical", "very high-tempo, man-marking all over the pitch and capable of very aggressive pressing". They play with a back three, but can switch to a back four in some matches [4]. Nathan Aké said the same: "A team that plays man-to-man and attacks... it will be a high-intensity game" [4].

That description has a tactical consequence. A man-marking side leaves big spaces once the first pass beats its press. Fenerbahçe's job is to break that first wave quickly and with few touches, and Ederson's distribution is a weapon here. Aké said his understanding with the goalkeeper is "getting better match by match", and included Milan Škriniar in that triangle [4]. Every clean build-up from the back can leave one Slavia player marking nobody in a pitch-wide man-to-man system.

Kartal's three plans and Greenwood's new role

Kartal said Fenerbahçe have prepared not one but three separate game plans for Slavia, to be switched on depending on how the match unfolds [4]. The most telling hint concerned Mason Greenwood. Kartal called him very creative in attack but admitted he may have defensive shortcomings, adding: "Tomorrow he could play in a different role" [5]. Our reading: freeing Greenwood partly from defensive duties and pulling him into a role where he receives the ball closer to the box, with team-mates covering the spaces he cannot track back into.

Anadolu Agency's predicted XI fits that frame: Ederson in goal; Semedo, Škriniar, Aké and Brown at the back; Kanté and Guendouzi in midfield; Greenwood, İrfan Can Kahveci and Oğuz Aydın ahead of them; Muriqi up front [2]. Kartal also left rotation decisions until the eve of the match, saying he would look at players' sleep, muscle fatigue and mental state [4].

Absentees and question marks

Fenerbahçe are definitely without two players: the injured Türkiye international Mert Müldür and Marco Asensio, who is due to have surgery next week [2]. Kartal called Asensio "very different, very valuable", and said Kanté still has pain but will play "as long as he is healthy" [5]. That matters: against Slavia's high-tempo press, Fenerbahçe's first line of defence in midfield depends on Kanté's legs.

Slavia's biggest news is disciplinary. Head coach Jindřich Trpišovský left experienced striker Tomáš Chorý out of the Istanbul squad after his red card for a gesture towards the referee in a 0-0 league draw with Hradec Králové: "He clearly crossed the line" [6]. UEFA also notes that Slavia's David Jurásek spent the first half of 2025/26 on loan at Beşiktaş; his last appearance there was a 2-1 Turkish Cup win at Fenerbahçe [3] — a player who knows Kadıköy.

Form: two teams, two moods

Fenerbahçe were wounded in Europe but recovered at home. The 6-0 at Villa Park went down, according to UEFA, as Aston Villa's biggest win in UEFA club competition [9]. Four days later came a 3-0 win over Alanyaspor, with goals from Lukaku, Kerem Aktürkoğlu and Vedat Muriqi [8]. In the official TFF table, Fenerbahçe have scored 22 and conceded only 6 in eight games and sit third on 16 points [7]. Kartal's verdict on the Villa defeat was blunt: "We had shortcomings, we identified them... tomorrow we can be tougher" [5].

Slavia lost 3-2 at home to Lens and 2-0 away at Sabah [2]. According to UEFA, Lens' win in Prague ended the French side's 16-match winless run away from home in Europe [9]. Their last league game also finished goalless, and the Chorý affair has dominated the build-up [6].

History and key players

According to UEFA, Fenerbahçe have lost only two of their last ten UEFA matches against Czech opposition (W5 D3) — both of them to Slavia in the 2021/22 Conference League knockout play-offs [3]. Kartal recalled that tie — "four years ago the same coach was there, we lost 3-2" — but insisted: "Tomorrow is not a revenge match, it's a Champions League match" [5]. The most recent meeting went Fenerbahçe's way, 2-1 in Prague in the 2024/25 Europa League [1]. Tonight is the club's 309th European match; the previous 308 produced 122 wins, 68 draws and 118 defeats [1].

The standout individual is Romelu Lukaku: two goals and two assists in his last three games against Slavia, and on the scoresheet against Alanyaspor [3][8]. But Anadolu's predicted XI has Muriqi leading the line [2], so whether Lukaku starts or arrives as part of a second-half plan will reveal which of Kartal's three set-ups opens the night.

Probabilities: what the numbers support

We do not quote betting odds, but the verified data point in a clear direction. UEFA's figures show Slavia have never won away in the Champions League (D4 L7) and have never won a game after conceding first (D1 L12) [3], while Fenerbahçe have never lost a home Champions League game in which they scored first [3]. On that basis the most likely scenario is a home win built on Fenerbahçe scoring first. Second is a draw in which Slavia's press keeps the game knotted for long spells. Least likely is a Slavia win — which would be the first away victory in the club's Champions League history. One caveat: Fenerbahçe have not led at half-time in any of their last 12 Champions League games [3], so patience may be the key word.

The European plan: why this is a must-win

Kartal did not hide the target: "If we take three points, we will have more hope for the top 24. On paper, there are more matches we should be able to win" [5]. That sentence sums up Fenerbahçe's league-phase strategy: points not taken against Roma and Aston Villa can only be recovered in fixtures like this one. A win would take them to four points and make the play-off maths realistic; dropping points would reduce the margin for error in the remaining matches to almost nothing.

The calendar matters too: six days after this game Fenerbahçe visit Galatasaray at Rams Park. Kartal's "we take it match by match" line [5] is right, but every change he makes tonight will also shape Monday.

Final word

On paper this is Fenerbahçe's match; but Slavia's man-to-man game is the kind that can crumple the paper inside half an hour. Kartal's three plans, Greenwood's new role and Kanté's knees will write tonight's answer. For the European table see Champions League standings; for the club hub see Fenerbahçe.
